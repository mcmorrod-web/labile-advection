# Tone and Style Reference

## Target Blend

- **Liu Cixin** — Hard systems thinking; civilisation-scale consequences rendered through individual experience; ideas that dwarf the characters but the characters still matter
- **Cormac McCarthy** — Spare, physical prose; landscape as moral presence; violence without commentary; dialogue stripped to bone
- **Silvia Park** — Family rupture and reunion rendered with precision and warmth; robots and AI treated as emotional subjects, not just plot devices; Ishiguro-esque attention to what's left unsaid; the human-machine boundary explored through relationship, not spectacle. *Luminous* (estranged siblings — two human, one robot — colliding around a murder investigation in unified Korea) mirrors this novel's core: separated siblings, AI consciousness, the question of what's human when the boundaries dissolve. Park's style is warm but controlled — not sentimental, not cold.
- **Adrian Tchaikovsky** — Non-human and mutant ecosystems treated with genuine curiosity; speculative biology as worldbuilding; large-cast multi-POV structure; evolution as narrative engine

## What This Means in Practice

- The prose should be lean but not cold — McCarthy's economy, not his nihilism
- World-systems (the mesh network, AI doctrine, ecological mutation) should feel as real and thought-through as Liu Cixin's physics
- Mutant species and ecological change should be treated with Tchaikovsky's naturalist eye — wonder and threat simultaneously
- The collectives should feel like real societies, not dystopia set dressing
- The journey across a surprisingly alive world allows for Tchaikovsky-style encounters with radically different ways of living
- Shanghai flashbacks can carry more density and sensory detail — the old world remembered in high resolution (collapse was recent, memories are vivid)

## Chapter Opening Device: Equipment Perspective

Every chapter begins with a short passage from the perspective of the equipment — the AI network watching the humans through its sensors, drones, robots, and infrastructure.

### Rules for Equipment Openings

- Written in a distinct voice: observational, non-emotional, precise, but not mechanical — more like a naturalist watching a species
- The equipment notices things humans don't: heat signatures, gait patterns, micro-expressions, atmospheric data, wear on materials, biological changes
- It does not use human moral categories — no judgment, no empathy, no cruelty, just attention
- Early chapters: these read as ambient, almost poetic scene-setting — the reader doesn't yet know what's speaking
- Middle chapters: subtle patterns emerge — the equipment seems to track specific people, anticipate behaviour, coordinate responses
- Late chapters (after the robot reveals the truth): the openings retroactively reframe — the reader now understands they've been reading the AI's experimental log all along
- The voice should feel like a third POV character: not Tian Hongpao, not Character 02, but the network itself
- The deepest layer: the AI is not just watching — it is comparing what it observes against what its simulations predicted, and annotating the discrepancies

### What the Equipment Might Observe

- A figure crossing a field at dawn (heat signature, respiratory rate, stride length)
- Drone flight paths and cargo weight anomalies
- Robot patrol patterns and the humans who interact with them
- Atmospheric contamination levels and species movement
- Gatherings, absences, route deviations, sleep patterns
- The slow accumulation of data that, to the AI, constitutes understanding
- Predictions: subtle language that implies expectation ("consistent with model", "deviation within threshold", "parameter adjustment scheduled") — these should be faint enough to miss on first read but unmistakable on reread
- Comparisons between collectives: a detail that only makes sense if the observer is tracking multiple populations simultaneously

### Seeded Openings (specific planned passages)

Some equipment openings are deliberate seeds that pay off later, read on first pass as ambient surveillance and recognised retrospectively when the later scene arrives.

- **The Four Hills, from the air.** An opening passage carrying an aerial view of Flagstaff Hill, Dalkey Hill, Killiney Hill, and Gorse Hill — granite, whin, a concrete obelisk on a wooded summit, the sea, wind direction, corrosion on a metal pole, the absence of people. Unlabelled. Unattributed. Placed in a non-Ireland chapter (China or Moon) well before the Ireland flight sequence. The reader reads it as one more equipment observation. Later, when the Ireland character launches a drone over the south Dublin hills at year 12 and the storage is pulled, the reader recognises that they have already read the footage. The opening was the flight. See `notes/ireland--south_dublin_hills.md`, "Seeded chapter opening" section.
  - Execution discipline: no tells on first read. The Killiney Obelisk is named only by form, not by name. No word "drone" or "footage" or "Ireland" in the passage. The recognition must be earned by the reader, not telegraphed.
  - If the deeper layer is taken (drone repair knowledge arrives over the Catholic / Wuyishan radio link), then this opening is simultaneously the drone's view, the AI network's view, and the novel's surveillance voice — the recursion fuses rather than stacking.

### Tone

- Early: reads like weather or landscape description — McCarthy-adjacent
- Middle: reads like field notes from a biologist — Tchaikovsky-adjacent
- Late: reads like intelligence briefings — Liu Cixin-adjacent
- The shift should be gradual enough that the reader can't pinpoint when it changed

## POV Voice Contrast

- Equipment openings: Non-human observation; precise, ambient, no moral framework; the reader slowly realises this is a third intelligence
- Tian Hongpao: Practical, observational, poetic only in private moments; systems thinker who notices mechanical and ecological detail
- Character 02 (Shanghai past): TBD — should offer a distinctly different register, tempo, and attention pattern
- Moon detective (framing voice): TBD — analytical, searching, humane. The person assembling the fragments. See below.

## Sentence-Level Style Guide

### Default Prose

- **Short declarative sentences as baseline.** McCarthy's influence: subject-verb-object. No filler. No "seemed to," "appeared to," "began to." If she crossed the field, she crossed the field.
- **Longer sentences only when landscape or memory demands it.** When the prose opens up — a valley at dawn, a Shanghai flashback, the mutation zone in bloom — the sentence length can stretch. But it earns the length through accumulated physical detail, not abstraction.
- **No comma splices creating breathless run-ons.** Period-heavy prose. Semicolons sparingly and deliberately.
- **Em dashes for interruption and pivot** — a thought broken by event, an observation that redirects mid-sentence.

### Exposition Handling (Liu Cixin method)

- Technical exposition delivered confidently, not apologetically. The drone courier system, the AI's experimental design, the contamination chemistry — explained through character engagement, not narrator asides.
- The reader is trusted. No "as you know" constructions. No pausing the story to explain the world.
- Systems and science woven into action: Tian Hongpao explains how a drone works while repairing one, not in a separate paragraph of background.

### Park's Warmth Register

- When the prose touches family, sibling memory, or the robot relationship — allow more warmth, more sensory softness. Park gives permission for tenderness without sentimentality.
- This is the counterweight to McCarthy's austerity. The novel needs both: the spare physical prose AND the moments where emotional precision replaces mechanical precision.
- The patrol robot scenes, the sibling memories, the reunion — these are Park territory. Let the prose breathe.

### Equipment Opening Prose

- Clipped, list-like observations. Short noun phrases. Data without interpretation.
- Some openings use machine-sensor language (heat signatures, coordinates, spectral analysis)
- Some use animal-perspective language (scent trails, ground-level thermal, predator-style tracking of movement) — these are the AI observing through implanted animals. The reader may notice the difference before understanding it. See `notes/china--implant_technology.md`.
- No human moral categories. No adjectives that imply judgment. "Elevated cortisol" not "frightened." "Deviation from predicted route" not "lost."

## Dialogue Conventions

### Quotation Marks

Decision needed: McCarthy uses none. Park and Tchaikovsky use them conventionally. This novel should decide once and commit. Options:
- No quotation marks (McCarthy-clean, but risks confusion in multi-speaker scenes)
- Quotation marks with minimal tags (conventional but reliable)
- Em-dash dialogue (continental European style — clean, distinctive)

### Tags and Silence

- Minimal tags. "Said" when needed. Nothing else. No "exclaimed," "muttered," "intoned."
- No adverb tags ever. Not "said quietly." If the reader needs to know it was quiet, the surrounding prose should make that obvious.
- Use silence. McCarthy's characters often say nothing when a response is expected. The space between lines of dialogue carries meaning. A question met with silence is an answer.

### Language and Translation

- Characters speak Mandarin, French, English, Irish, Shan, and other languages. The novel is rendered in English.
- When a character switches language, acknowledge it in narration ("She said something in Shan that made the others laugh") rather than dropping in untranslated words for decoration.
- Occasional untranslated words only when the POV character doesn't understand them — the reader shares the character's incomprehension.
- The collective's Catholic-Mao doctrinal language should have its own verbal register: formal, slightly archaic, rhythmic. When characters quote doctrine, it should sound like scripture — because that's what it is to them.

### Equipment Openings

- No dialogue. The AI does not speak in human language. The implanted animals do not think in words. Observation only.

## Landscape and Environment Prose

### McCarthy's Method: Landscape as Moral Presence

- The physical world is not backdrop. It acts on the characters. Weather is not described — it is endured. Terrain is not observed — it is crossed.
- Contamination is invisible. The danger is in what grows wrong, what's absent (no birds in certain zones, no insects, silence where there should be sound), what the body does over time (chronic cough, skin lesions, teeth loosening).
- Ireland: Atlantic weather as constant presence. Rain that doesn't stop. Wind that shapes posture. Sea light that changes the colour of everything.

### Tchaikovsky's Method: Naturalist's Eye

- Mutant species described with genuine scientific curiosity. Morphology, behaviour, habitat. A new organism is not a horror — it is a specimen. Wonder and threat simultaneously.
- The mutation zone is an ecology, not a wasteland. Things grow. Things hunt. Things reproduce in ways that weren't possible before. The AI is watching this with as much attention as it watches the human collectives.
- Implanted animals in the mutation zone: the AI's biological field instruments. Some of these animals may themselves be changing. The intersection of implant technology and contamination-driven evolution is producing something genuinely novel. Describe it the way Tchaikovsky would — with fascination, not revulsion.

### Terrain as Journey Structure

- The 2,400km journey across southern China passes through radically different landscapes. Each section should feel physically distinct:
  - Fujian mountains: steep, forested, mist, narrow valleys
  - Hunan/Jiangxi lowlands: river basins, flat, open, exposed
  - Guizhou/Guangxi: karst plateau, strange rock formations, deep gorges
  - Yunnan plateau: high, dry, expansive, cold at night
  - Xishuangbanna: sudden tropical drop, humid, dense vegetation, different smell
  - Shan State mountains: steep again, jungle, border territory

### Ireland Landscape

- Wetter, greener, smaller scale than China. Everything is closer together.
- The contamination band is not a wall — it's a gradient you walk into. The grass looks the same. The soil looks the same. The danger is invisible and accumulated.
- The Atlantic coast: exposed rock, horizontal rain, salt on everything. The west coast settlements where northern refugees were turned away are bleak, windswept places.
- French Cork: more sheltered, more fertile, more built-up. The Blackwater Valley has a different quality of light.

## Flashback / Memory Style (Shanghai)

### Why It Feels Different

Tian Hongpao's Shanghai memories are only 12 years old. She was a teenager. The old world is vivid, raw, and intrusive. These are not faded recollections — they are high-resolution sensory records that break into the present uninvited.

### Prose Rules for Flashback

- **Higher sensory density**: Sounds, smells, textures, light quality, the temperature of surfaces. The old world remembered through the body, not the mind.
- **Longer sentences, more fluid rhythm**: Memory flows. The present is angular and declarative; the past is more liquid, more continuous. Sentences can run longer because memory doesn't have the present's urgency to be precise.
- **Involuntary intrusion**: Memories break into present narrative triggered by sensory echoes — a smell, a quality of light, the sound of water, a texture under the hand. The transition should feel like ambush, not decision.
- **Decay over time**: If memories recur, they should subtly change. Details lost, others sharpened. A face that was clear is now blurred. A street name she's not sure of anymore. Memory is not a recording — it's a reconstruction that deteriorates.
- **The sibling**: Memories of the younger sibling carry the most emotional charge. Handle with Park's precision — specific, physical, unsentimental but devastating. The smell of their hair. The sound of their laugh from another room. The last time she saw them.

## Ireland Sections Voice

### Prose Register

- Wetter, slower, more lateral than the China sections. Atlantic rhythm — longer breaths, more weather in the prose.
- The Ireland character's internal voice (TBD, but initial direction): reflective, art-inflected, possibly more literary than Tian Hongpao's practical register. He processes the world through aesthetic categories — light, colour, composition — because of the art practice.
- This creates a genuine voice contrast: Tian Hongpao sees machines and systems; the Ireland character sees images and patterns.

### Craft Rule: Withhold the Image (Footage Scenes)

The Ireland character keeps and watches pre-collapse video footage of his childhood ground (the Four Hills Walk — see `notes/ireland--south_dublin_hills.md`). Any later year-12 footage — a drone flyby if it happens, any other recovered video — is subject to the same rule:

- **The content of the image on the screen is never rendered on the page.**
- The prose describes the room around the screen — face, hands, weight of the device, speaker sound, pause, rewind, turn-off — never the image.
- If the reader needs to know what's on a clip, they infer it from what the character does. Content of the image is always offstage.
- Sebald's discipline: the picture is there; the text does not explain it. The reader does the looking.
- This rule applies to every footage-watching scene in the Ireland strand and to any recovered pre-collapse video in other strands as well.

The discipline protects the scene from the sentimentality traps of the before/after montage, the film-elegy register, and the tech-spectacle drone shot. Held offstage, the image retains the unbearable specificity the reader builds for themselves.

### Institutional Speech

- The Catholic network's language: institutional, doctrinal, with an undertow of control. Echoes of real Irish institutional speech patterns — the parish announcement, the school principal's address, the confession booth's structured exchange.
- When network figures speak, their language should carry the weight of authority that has survived the end of the world. They speak as if the institution is eternal, because to them it is.
- The sectarian undertow: the network's language implicitly excludes. It defines community by who belongs. The northern Protestant refugees are outside this language.

### French Cork

- When the story touches French-controlled territory: a different texture. More crowded, more polyglot, more urban. French and English and northern Irish dialect layered on top of each other.
- The northern refugees integrated into French Cork have their own emerging pidgin — French-English-Ulster hybrid. This is 12 years of living together producing something new.

## Pacing and Structure

### Moon Detective as Framing Device

The novel is structured as an investigation/reconstruction by a detective on a lunar base, piecing together what happened on Earth from fragmentary data. See `characters/moon--detective.md`.

- The framing creates distance: everything the reader experiences on Earth is mediated — it has been found, selected, and assembled by someone trying to understand.
- This reframes the equipment openings: they may be data the detective is reviewing. The "naturalist watching a species" voice gains another layer — it could be the detective's analytical notes on AI sensor data.
- The framing should be light, not heavy. Brief interstitial sections — a sentence or two of the detective's perspective between major narrative blocks. A prologue that establishes the lunar viewpoint. An epilogue that answers (or refuses to answer) the detective's central question: is Earth recovering, or dying?
- The detective embodies the Liu Cixin thematic question: humanity must overcome this catastrophic period to become something stronger. The detective is watching to find out if it's happening.

### Chapter Length and Rhythm

- Aim for varied chapter length. Short chapters for crisis and movement. Longer chapters for settlement and relationship.
- The equipment openings provide a consistent beat — a moment of distance before each chapter's human action. They should be short (half a page to a page) and not compete with the main narrative for space.

### Scene Transitions

- Hard cuts between POVs. No bridging narration. The reader jumps from Tian Hongpao in Fujian to the Ireland character in Kerry to Character 02 in Tungapuri.
- White space between scenes within a chapter. Time jumps within a POV signalled by white space plus a grounding detail (time of day, weather, a physical action) in the first sentence of the new scene.

### Journey Pacing

- The Long March across China must avoid both montage-blur and day-by-day tedium.
- Tchaikovsky's method in *Children of Time*: time jumps between significant encounters. Weeks or months can pass in white space. What matters is the encounters — with other collectives, with outside communities, with hunter-killers, with the landscape itself.
- The journey should feel long without reading long. A handful of deeply rendered encounters spaced across the distance, with the terrain changing between them to signal that time and miles have passed.

## Anti-Patterns

### What This Novel Should NOT Do

**Genre traps:**
- AI-as-villain reveal. The AI is not evil. It may not be benign. It is conducting experiments on humans for reasons that may be unknowable. Resist the temptation to make it a villain.
- Post-apocalypse poverty porn. The world is damaged but alive. People make art, fall in love, cook food, tell jokes. Survival is not the only mode.
- Noble savage communities. The collectives and outside settlements are real societies with real politics, corruption, kindness, and stupidity. Nobody is pure.
- Tech-solutionism. The implants, the AI, the drones — none of these save anyone. Technology is a condition, not a solution.

**Prose traps:**
- Over-explaining the world to the reader. Trust them. If the contamination band is mentioned in passing, the reader will figure out what it means from context.
- Characters who think in exposition. Tian Hongpao does not think "As I walked through the collective, which was one of many in a mesh network controlled by a hidden AI..." She thinks about the drone she needs to fix.
- Dialogue as information delivery. People don't explain things to each other that they both already know.

**Emotional traps:**
- Sentimentalising the collapse. The old world was not a paradise. Shanghai was not better — it was different. Nostalgia is a character trait (Tian Hongpao's), not a narrative position.
- Making survival romantic. Living without electricity, medical care, or safe food is not noble. It's exhausting and often ugly.
- The "chosen one" exile. Tian Hongpao is not special because fate chose her. She is exiled because she is inconvenient. The AI may have chosen her — but that makes her a variable, not a hero.

**Author-specific traps:**
- McCarthy: nihilism for its own sake. Suffering without purpose. Women as symbols rather than characters. (This novel has a lesbian woman as lead — it cannot fall into McCarthy's gender blindness.)
- Liu Cixin: characters as vessels for ideas rather than people. Lectures disguised as dialogue. Women as rewards or decorations. (Same corrective applies.)
- Tchaikovsky: over-elaborating speculative biology at the expense of human story. The mutation zone is fascinating but it serves the characters, not the other way around.
- Park: allowing the robot/AI emotional thread to overwhelm the human relationships. The implant technology is a condition of this world, not the point of it.

**Structural traps:**
- The Ireland sections feeling like a B-plot. They must carry equal weight — different texture, different rhythm, but equal importance.
- The equipment openings becoming a gimmick. If they don't deepen the story on every read, they're decorative.
- The moon detective framing becoming intrusive. Light touch. The detective's presence should be felt, not heard at length.
