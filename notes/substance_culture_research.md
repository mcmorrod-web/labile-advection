## Premise

A damaged world is a thirsty one. Twelve years after the collapse, every surviving community has rebuilt some version of the consolation economy that pre-existed the state. What changes is the chemistry, the supply chain, and who controls it. The drink gets harder. The smoke gets rougher. Pre-collapse pharmaceutical stockpiles are mostly gone, and what replaces them is improvised, regional, and politically loaded.

This is not background colour. Substance use is one of the few continuities between the old world and the new — the body's demands didn't end with the grid.

## Why "Harder" Is the Post-Collapse Default

### Beer and wine fail; spirits don't

- No reliable refrigeration outside specific contexts (cellars, mountain caves, the lunar base's cold-store). Beer spoils. Wine in unsterile vessels turns within months.
- Spirits keep indefinitely. They tolerate heat, light, and bad cork.
- Distillation concentrates calories and alcohol into a fraction of the volume. Trade economics favour the still.

### Spirits double as infrastructure

- Antiseptic for wounds and surgical tools.
- Anaesthetic of last resort.
- Fuel for lamps and small spark-ignition engines (see `ireland--fuel_research.md`).
- Solvent, preservative, currency.

### Distillation is the cheapest morale technology in the world

- The knowledge is centuries deep in every region of this novel — baijiu in China, poitín in Ireland, eau-de-vie in France, lao khao across the Shan borderlands.
- A still is two pots and a pipe. Anyone who fixed a kettle as a child can build one.
- When the state retreats, the still arrives.

### Historical analogues

- Prohibition US, post-Soviet Russia, post-Yugoslav Bosnia, every famine-era rural economy: the consistent pattern is consolidation toward spirits, criminalisation followed by tolerance, and a parallel currency emerging around bottles.

### One major exception: tea

- The "harder" consolidation describes alcohol. It does not describe stimulants.
- Tea is the substance that survives the collapse most cleanly in any region where the bushes grow. It is perennial, requires no industrial input, and stays useful indefinitely once dried. The Wuyishan collective sits in one of the world's great tea regions and effectively retains its caffeine economy intact.
- The Catholic safe zone has lost its caffeine entirely — no coffee, no tea, no domestic source. This is one of the sharpest cultural asymmetries in the novel and deserves its own structural attention. See the China and Ireland sections below.

## China — Wuyishan Collective

### Tea — the central substance

- Wuyishan is one of the most famous tea regions in the world. The mountain produces Lapsang Souchong, Wuyi rock oolongs, and Da Hong Pao — the Big Red Robe, whose original mother trees on the cliffs are over four hundred years old.
- Tea bushes are perennial. They do not die without electricity. The collective sits inside a tea industry that survived the collapse intact in everything except export.
- This is the most important asymmetry between the China and Ireland strands: **the collective has caffeine.** The Catholic safe zone does not (see below). The morning ritual in the collective is a hot cup that does what the Irish substitute teas cannot.
- Tea is doctrinally clean. The Catholic-Mao register has no tradition of moralising it; both halves of the parentage treat it as appropriate, even virtuous. Tea is poured at confession-style sessions, at tribunals, at funerals, at leadership meetings. It is the substance the doctrine actively endorses.
- Tea is a primary trade good in the inter-collective drone-courier network. A pouch of high-grade Wuyi rock oolong moves further than any spirit; it has higher value-to-weight than baijiu and less doctrinal friction than tobacco.
- The Da Hong Pao mother trees, if they survived, are now post-collapse cultural relics of immense local significance. A few grams of leaf from those trees is the highest-value object the collective produces. Whether it is traded or hoarded is a leadership-level decision.
- Tian Hongpao's name shares characters with the tea cultivar. She is named for the place she lives. This is a small fact that may matter at chapter-opening level.

### Official position (alcohol)

- The Catholic-Mao doctrine moralises drunkenness using both halves of its parentage. McQuaid-flavoured temperance language ("a sober house, a sober heart") fused with self-criticism's labour-purification rhetoric ("the bottle steals the hand from the hoe").
- Public drunkenness is sin-category, surfaced in confession-style reporting sessions.
- Reproduction-track residents are held to stricter standards than older labourers.

### Practical reality

- The leader, a Party operator wearing a cassock, runs a regulated ration system in everything but name. Festival days, harvest completion, returning patrol crews, end-of-cycle confession — alcohol appears, and disappears, on schedule.
- A parallel black-market still exists behind the official one. The leader knows. The leader lets it. It functions as a pressure valve and as an informant net — the people running the unofficial still owe him their continued breathing.

### Domestic alcohol production

- **Baijiu** from sorghum and rice. Distillation knowledge is universal across China; Wuyishan is not historically a baijiu region (Sichuan and Guizhou are) but the technique travels. Quality is uneven; the better runs go to leadership tables and to drone-courier exchanges.
- **Huangjiu (yellow rice wine)** and **mijiu (sweet rice wine)** at household scale. Lower alcohol, doctrinally invisible, treated as cooking and ceremony rather than drink.
- **Fruit wines and infusions**: bayberry (yang mei jiu), plum (mei jiu), persimmon, lychee where the lower elevations support it. Subtropical southern Chinese tradition.
- **Tea-baijiu fusions** are the regional specialty the collective has effectively invented at twelve years — Wuyi oolong infused into baijiu, aged in clay. A bottle of this is a leadership-grade gift in the courier network. It is also one of the few pleasures that carries authentic local pride.
- **Snake wine, lizard wine, scorpion wine** — medicinal infusions of small animals in spirit. Folk tradition. Persists as a private and slightly suspect practice.

### Tobacco

- Pre-collapse China was a tobacco superpower. Yunnan was the country's largest producer, with the Hongta Group running one of the world's biggest cigarette industries before the collapse. The plant suits the climate of southern China at large.
- The Wuyishan collective grows tobacco on south-facing terraces; quality is mediocre but the cultivation is real. Better leaf comes north through the inter-collective network from Yunnan, where the climate and tradition are stronger.
- Hand-rolled, pipe, **bamboo water-pipes (shuǐyāndǒu)** for the older men. Occasional cigarette stubs traded back and forth among the most senior cadres. Pre-collapse industrial cigarettes are exhausted, but the form lingers in memory and in the gestures of older smokers.
- Smoking is a status marker. Younger residents on duty tracks are forbidden it; older labourers and committee members enjoy it openly. The cigarette is rank.

### Pharmacopoeia and stimulants

- The Wuyishan region has deep traditional Chinese medicine traditions. The collective's medic and the older women among the labourers retain working pharmacopoeia knowledge.
- **Ephedra (麻黄, mahuang)** is the structurally important one: a natural source of ephedrine and pseudoephedrine, used in TCM for asthma and respiratory illness. It is a real stimulant. It does not grow in subtropical Fujian (it is a northern Chinese plant) but moves through the inter-collective network as a medicinal good. Recreational use exists at the edges and is not officially recognised.
- **Ginseng, astragalus, schisandra (五味子, wǔ wèi zǐ — Wuyi/Fujian native), goji, licorice, ginger.** A working herbal tradition that does much of the work pharmaceutical salvage no longer can.
- **Lingzhi (reishi)** and **dong chong xia cao (cordyceps)** are cultivated and foraged. Not psychoactive; ritual-medicinal.
- **Opium poppy** was historically grown across China before the early 20th-century eradication campaigns. The plant is still found in old gardens and isolated valleys. The collective does not officially cultivate it. Whether the unofficial still-runners or the medic also tend a few plants behind a wall is the kind of question the leader prefers not to ask.

### Hard drugs

- Officially absent. Doctrinal anathema.
- Practically: opium passes through the drone-courier network in small medical quantities, originating from the Yunnan–Shan State supply chain (see Tungapuri below). The collective's medic has access. Nobody else is supposed to.
- Methamphetamine appears occasionally in the hands of long-distance traders crossing from the southwest. The pre-collapse Chinese chemical industry was a major precursor source for the Shan State labs; that industry is gone, so the post-collapse meth running through the inter-collective region comes from Tungapuri's cruder synthesis routes, not from Chinese factories.
- The collective's response to meth is uniformly punitive — tribunal, ration cut, banishment in extreme cases. This is one of the cleaner sin-categories: easy to enforce, hard to defend.

### Tian Hongpao's relationship

- A drinker on her terms. She is not a problem; she is also not abstinent. Baijiu at festivals, occasional pulls from a hidden bottle in the maintenance shed.
- She does not smoke. The chronic respiratory damage from contamination exposure (continuity log) makes it medically untenable, which she experiences as one more theft.
- She has never used opium and she has watched what it does to the medic.

## China — Tungapuri / Eastern Shan State

### Pre-collapse baseline

- The Golden Triangle's twenty-first-century reincarnation: opium and methamphetamine production zones, armed ethnic militias, Chinese telecom-fraud compounds (see `map_descriptions.md`).
- Post-collapse: the smuggling networks *became* the governance structure. There was no transition; the state's absence simply revealed which institutions had been doing the actual work.

### Substance economy as polity

- The Tungapuri authorities — whatever exact configuration of militias, fixers, and former compound bosses now run the place — derive a substantial portion of their tax base from opium and methamphetamine.
- Opium: pain management for a population without antibiotics or surgery; palliative care for the dying; trade good moving north into China and west into India along refugee corridors.
- Methamphetamine: labour discipline. Border patrols, mine work, long-haul portage. The Shan State's pre-collapse production capacity was vast; twelve years on, the labs run on cruder synthesis but the supply has not stopped.
- Cannabis grows wild in the lower elevations. Universally available, untaxed, socially unmarked.

### Authority and addiction

- The militias do not moralise the trade because they cannot afford to. Their fighters use; their fighters' families use; their fighters' fighters' families are paid in kind.
- Addiction-management is part of how authority is exercised. A militia chief's leverage over his men runs through their habits.
- Public violence around supply disputes is the texture of disorder when it occurs. It is not constant. Most days nothing happens.

### Alcohol

- Lao khao (Thai/Lao rice spirit) and Shan rice wine are everywhere. Tobacco is endemic; betel quid is more common than tobacco in the older population.
- Drinking is unmarked. The doctrinal weight that exists in the Wuyishan collective does not exist here. Nobody confesses to a glass of anything.

### Character 02's daily texture

- Lives inside this economy. Cannot be neutral about it; cannot be in opposition to it; runs the BnB-network logistics that the substance trade depends on for movement.
- This is the precise structural difference from Tian Hongpao — same world, different chemistry. One sister wakes up to bell and ration; the other wakes up to the smell of rice spirit and someone owing someone money.
- The scene where the two finally reunite has to register this difference physically before either of them speaks.

## China — The Long March

### Trade goods of the road

- Spirits and tobacco are the universal currencies between collectives and outside settlements. Cleaner than printed paper. Lighter than rice. Universally recognised.
- A traveller without something to share is suspect. Showing up at a settlement with a small flask and a tobacco pouch is closer to passport than gift.

### Regional variation as journey marker

- Fujian: rice spirit, oolong-leaf tobacco substitutes, mountain herbs.
- Hunan/Jiangxi: stronger sorghum baijiu, harsh and cheap.
- Guizhou/Guangxi: maize spirit; betel quid appearing as the latitudes drop.
- Yunnan: tea-stem smoke, fermented mare's milk in the higher communities, opium tolerance increasing as the southwest approaches. **Yunnan coffee** — pre-collapse Yunnan was developing as a Chinese coffee-producing region, with significant plantations around Pu'er and Baoshan. Some partially survive. Coffee is rare, regional, and unmistakably Yunnan; Tian Hongpao's first cup of it on the journey would be one of the few moments where she encounters something the collective entirely lacks.
- Xishuangbanna and beyond: the Shan State economy. The change is unmistakable.
- Each section's substance vocabulary is part of how Tchaikovsky-style "you have crossed a border" gets registered without exposition.

### Hunter-killer territory

- A bottle does not buy passage from a hunter-killer. The environmental hazard is chemistry-blind.
- But: settlements that survive in hunter-killer corridors often have unusually robust substance economies — concentrated coping, concentrated trade, concentrated information about who is moving where.

## Ireland — Catholic Safe Zone (Kerry / Clare)

### Domestic alcohol production

The safe zone has every input it needs and centuries of practice. This is the part of the substance economy that is not a problem.

- **Stout and dark ale** from roasted barley. Pre-collapse Ireland's signature drink. Even a crude post-collapse version reads as Irish in a way other beers don't. Hops grow in Ireland. Storage is short — this is the everyday drink, brewed and drunk fast.
- **Cider** from Kerry/Cork orchards where they survived. Hard cider keeps better than beer. **Perry** from pears, smaller scale.
- **Mead and melomel** from honey. Bee populations may be healthier post-collapse (no agricultural pesticides). Mead is the highest-status fermented drink — long history, special-occasion, doctrinally clean.
- **Heather ale** from Kerry uplands. Distinctive enough to be regional identity.
- **Fruit wines**: elderberry, blackberry, gooseberry, rhubarb, dandelion, sloe. Low alcohol, household-scale. The wine on the kitchen table that isn't really wine.
- **Poitín** as the workhorse spirit (see `ireland--fuel_research.md` §10). The bog still, the cousin who runs it, the priest who knows and says nothing — all of this is pre-collapse Irish vernacular and survives intact. Distilled from grain, potato, sugar beet, fruit. Quality runs from clean rural-trade-grade product to contaminated batches that blind people. Methanol is the village problem.
- **Apple, pear, and sloe brandies** — fruit eau-de-vie, easy and good.
- **Whiskey** is the structurally interesting one. The knowledge is universal; the catch is *aging*. Real whiskey needs years in oak. At year 12, only spirit laid down in years 1–4 is properly mature. The pre-collapse distilleries' bonded warehouses (Midleton, Cooley, the like) held vast stocks of aged whiskey at the moment of collapse — much has been consumed, traded, or guarded over the intervening years. **A surviving bonded warehouse with mature stock is a strategic asset.** Whoever holds it has a genuinely irreplaceable trade good.

What they cannot make: wine in any quantity. Grapes don't ripen reliably in Ireland's climate. Wine in the safe zone is either French Cork's grape-substitute production (apple/grain "wine" given French names) or rare salvage.

### Network attitude

- The Catholic network publicly moralises drunkenness. The institutional language is McQuaid-direct: drink as the Irishman's curse, the wife at home, the child unfed.
- Privately, the network operates on the same understanding it has always operated on. The parish poitín economy is tolerated, sometimes participated in, occasionally exploited as leverage.
- This is not hypocrisy from inside the institution's frame. It is *order*. The public sermon and the private bottle do different jobs.

### Tobacco — the climate problem

- Tobacco is the hard problem. The climate is wrong: cool, wet, short summers. A few enthusiasts grow it in polytunnels and warm sheltered plots in Kerry/west Cork — yields poor, curing harder than growing. Domestic Irish tobacco at year 12 is a curiosity, not a crop.
- Pre-collapse salvage stockpiles ran out in the first three or four years. What remains is rare, valuable, and ceremonial.
- Substitute smokables that actually carry the smoking habit: **mugwort** (abundant wild, mild), **coltsfoot** (traditional, throat-coating), **mullein** (mild, smooth), **yarrow**, **heather**, **wild lettuce** (mildly sedative, opiate-adjacent), **industrial hemp leaf**. Most smokers use mixtures — the parish smoke is rarely a single herb.
- The priest's pipe is a status object precisely because the contents are real. The smell of Irish smoking at twelve years is not the smell of tobacco.

### Hard drugs — the depleted-pharmaceuticals problem

- This is the sharpest opportunity in the Irish strand and is not currently exploited in any other note.
- Year 1–4: pharmaceutical salvage from Dublin and Cork hospitals reached the safe zone in significant quantities. Opiates (morphine, oxycodone), benzodiazepines, antibiotics, insulin.
- Year 4–8: stocks visibly draining. Distribution becomes a network function. Who gets the morphine becomes a question with a parish answer.
- Year 8–12: opiate and benzo stocks largely exhausted. A population of people who got addicted in the early years cannot get supply.
- The texture of this is the priest's brother who has been dry-sick for six years; the Kerry fishing village with three people who do not come outside any more; the woman who walks to the parish house every Sunday for the prayer that used to come with a tablet and now only comes with the prayer.
- This gives the network another lever of authority. Control of the last pharmaceutical reserves — kept for "real medical need" — is one of the things that keeps the network powerful.

### Domestic opium — the parish poppy garden

- *Papaver somniferum* grows in Irish gardens. The seed is in every herbalist garden and every grandmother's flower bed. Cultivation knowledge is trivial: in any pre-modern herbalist text.
- Historical precedent: the Norfolk Fens grew opium poppies commercially in the 18th–19th centuries, supplying British domestic opium production (laudanum, paregoric). Climate-feasible.
- Yields are far lower than Afghan/Burmese production: Irish climate produces less alkaloid concentration than hot dry Asian conditions. A skilled gardener with a few hundred poppies produces small quantities of crude opium — parish-scale, not polity-scale.
- Processing into crude morphine requires basic chemistry (lime, ammonia, ethanol — all locally available). Pharmaceutical-grade refinement is not achievable.
- What this gives the world:
  - Partial year-12 relief for the long-term-addict population whose pharmaceutical supply ran out.
  - A new lever for the Catholic network: who runs the parish poppy garden, who brews the parish tincture, who gets it. Opiate distribution moves explicitly inside the institutional economy in a way pharmaceutical salvage was not.
  - A plant-level thematic connection between the Catholic safe zone and Tungapuri. Both economies grow opium. The scale and the ethics differ. The plant is the same.
- French Cork: the more sheltered climate and military authority of the French zone make larger-scale poppy cultivation plausible there too. The two zones could end up with competing opium economies — a genuinely new political fact in Ireland at year 12.
- Risk surface: methanol-blinded poitín is the village's textbook poisoning; ergotism is its accidental hallucinogen; bad parish opium tincture would be a third entry on the same list.

### Homegrown and wild

- **Cannabis** is essentially free. Outdoor cultivation is marginal but possible in south-facing Kerry and west Cork plots; mild winters help. The real answer is **polytunnels** — pre-collapse Irish horticulture used them at scale (Kerry, Cork, north Tipperary). Many would still be standing at year 12, patched and repaired. Anyone with a polytunnel can grow more cannabis than they need. Officially illegal in network-administered townships, practically tolerated in Kerry. The Catholic network's position is class-marked: the working farmer's smoke vs the priest's pipe.
- **Wild psilocybin mushrooms** (*Psilocybe semilanceata*, Liberty Caps) grow in Irish pasture every autumn. Always have. Pre-collapse this was a curiosity; post-collapse it is occasionally medicinal, occasionally recreational, occasionally framed as spiritual experience. Seasonal, free, weather-dependent, and folkloric — doctrinally and politically below the radar.
- **Fly agaric** (*Amanita muscaria*) grows in Irish woodland. Dissociative, toxic if mishandled, a real folk-tradition substance.
- **Datura, henbane**: dangerous deliriants. Old witchcraft-tradition plants. Real and occasionally fatal.
- **Ergot poisoning** from stored rye is a real risk in chaotic homesteads. Some visions reported in the parish are genuine ergotism, not faith. Year-12 grain-storage practices vary; episodes are plausible.
- **Plant medicine**: willow bark (salicylic acid, the pre-aspirin painkiller), foxglove (digitalis, lethal if dosed wrong), St John's Wort (mild antidepressant, real), valerian (sedative), garlic and honey for wounds, comfrey topical, yarrow for clotting. A working herbalist tradition that does the work pharmaceutical salvage no longer can — though it cannot replace antibiotics. People die at year 12 of infections that would have been routine pre-collapse.

### The caffeine void

- No domestic source. Coffee cannot be grown in Ireland or anywhere in Western Europe. Tea cannot be grown in Ireland. None of the wild Irish plants contain caffeine.
- Pre-collapse stockpiles (sealed coffee beans, tea, instant) are largely exhausted at year 12 but not entirely. A sealed jar of coffee is a luxury object — bartered, hoarded, given as a gift that means something.
- **Substitute "teas"**: dandelion root, chicory, roasted barley, rosehip, nettle, mint, meadowsweet, elderflower. These are warming and ritual-shaped but contain no caffeine.
- The cultural shift this implies: a population that has detoxed from caffeine over twelve years. Mornings are slower. The morning ritual is the same shape (a hot drink, a warming up, a small social moment) but the chemistry is gone.
- This is a sharp asymmetry with the Wuyishan collective, which sits inside a tea industry that survived the collapse. A traveller from the safe zone who reaches Wuyishan would notice immediately. A traveller in the other direction would notice the absence.

### Ireland character

- An artist living in the safe zone, processing the world through aesthetic categories. He drinks. He smokes when there is something to smoke. He is not an addict and is not pure.
- His relationship to the parish poitín economy is one of the textures that establishes his standing in the community — who shares with him, who does not.

## Ireland — French Cork

### French drinking culture, Irish raw materials

- French drinking culture survives the people. Cut off from grapes (the Mediterranean is a contaminated semi-enclosed sea — see `ireland--france_europe_fallout_research.md`), the French refugees distil what Ireland gives them.
- Apple eau-de-vie, grain spirit, potato spirit. Each is given a French name and treated as a wine substitute on the table.
- Cider, where the orchards survived. Northern-Irish-refugee homebrews. A cross-pollinated drinking culture that is genuinely new at twelve years.

### Cork as trading port

- Cork Harbour is the substance trade's gateway to the safe zone. Whatever moves in and out of Ireland passes through here.
- Tobacco runs through Cork because Cork is where tobacco can plausibly arrive — North African or South American salvage, occasionally fresh-grown from a southern Iberian survivor community.
- The polyglot Cork pidgin (`ireland--french_occupation.md`) carries the substance vocabulary too: a French-English-Ulster hybrid where the word for a drink, a smoke, a high all carry traces of three languages.

### Hard drugs

- Cork's hospital pharmaceutical stockpiles were larger and were defended by the French military authority. They have lasted longer than the Catholic-zone stocks. They are also closer to running out than the French want to admit.
- Cork's port economy means heroin and methamphetamine occasionally arrive on small craft from points unknown. The French authority's response is selective enforcement: against rivals, not against allies.
- The northern-refugee population integrated into Cork has its own pharmaceutical-trauma history from the Troubles era and the early collapse years. This is part of the hybrid-community texture.

## The Lunar Base

### Closed-loop, twelve years in

- Skeleton crew on a permanent installation. Closed-loop life support means *everything* is recycled, and everything is rationed.
- The base was not provisioned for indefinite habitation. Original supply runs ended at the collapse. Twelve years of attrition.

### What survives

- Someone is distilling something. Probably from algae sugars or fermented food-system byproducts. Scale: small. Quality: improving slowly. Distribution: informal.
- Tobacco does not exist on the moon. It has not existed on the moon for years. Nicotine-replacement patches from the medical stocks ran out in year three. Withdrawal stories are part of the base's oral history.
- Pharmaceutical stocks are managed by the medical officer with a precision that the Irish parish would recognise. The detective has access on case-by-case grounds.

### A scene the detective passes through

- Someone in a maintenance bay with a tube and a bottle, brewing something the official manifest does not list.
- The detective notes this and does not act on it. The texture of the base is partly that everyone on it is doing one thing they should not be doing, and that this is the price of twelve years.

### Tone register

- Cold, brief, almost domestic. The moon's substance economy is small enough to be a single character with a still.

## Cross-Cutting: The Implant Interlock

This is where the substance research stops being decorative and starts doing structural work.

### The doctrine's interpretive grid already exists

- `china--implant_technology.md` establishes that damaged neural implants can produce hallucinations, voices, motor glitches, seizures.
- The Catholic-Mao doctrine has explanations for "people who see things and hear voices" — and none of those explanations involve radiation-damaged hardware.
- A person is classified by the parish or the committee as: mentally ill, spiritually tested, possessed, contamination-sick, or unreliable.

### Add a substance economy on top

In the Wuyishan collective, on the journey, in Tungapuri, and in Catholic Ireland, the population now contains people whose state at any given moment may be:

- Hearing the AI through a working implant.
- Hallucinating from a damaged implant.
- Tripping on something a trader brought through.
- Mid-DT from a poitín binge or methadone-equivalent withdrawal.
- Ergotism from bad rye.
- Suffering one of the dozen ordinary pre-modern psychiatric conditions that humans have always suffered.
- Under doctrinal "spiritual testing" prescribed by the parish or the committee.

### What this gives the novel

- The reader cannot easily distinguish these states. Neither can the characters. Neither, sometimes, can the AI.
- The doctrine flattens all of them into the same category for governance purposes.
- The substance economy is one of the layers of plausible deniability that lets the AI's signal hide inside the noise. A person who hears a voice in this world has a dozen possible reasons to.
- This is the kind of unstable epistemic ground the tone guide explicitly wants. The reader is meant to feel the indeterminacy without being told it is indeterminacy.

### Specific scene possibilities

- A confession-style session in which a labourer reports voices. The leader treats it as spiritual testing. The medic suspects implant damage. The AI, listening, recognises its own signal pattern. The labourer himself does not know that he is also drunk.
- A Kerry villager whose ergot-poisoning visions are taken by the parish as authentic religious experience and whose pre-collapse implant is what is actually triggering them.
- Tian Hongpao on the road, sharing baijiu with a stranger who begins to hear the patrol robots before they arrive. Is the stranger implanted? Is she psychic? Is she drunk? Is the AI testing how the news reaches Tian Hongpao? All four can be true.

## Anti-Pattern Check

Drink and drugs handled badly become either grimdark texture or noble-suffering wallpaper. The tone guide's anti-patterns apply directly:

- **No poverty porn.** The drinking, the smoking, the using — these are not signs that the world is bad. People drank and smoked and used in the old world too. The chemistry has changed; the human practice has not.
- **No noble-savage communities.** The Tungapuri militias are not romantic outlaws. The Kerry parish is not a folk-paradise. The collectives are not peasant utopias. Substance use is a real political and economic fact in each of them, with real consequences for real people.
- **No moral framing in the prose.** Render the physical fact — the smell of a still, the priest's stained fingers, the militia chief's pinned pupils, the labourer's tremor — without commentary. Let the reader assemble the moral picture.
- **Park's warmth register is for the moments where someone shares a drink and means it.** The reunion. The patrol-robot scene. The Shanghai memory of a father pouring something at a wedding. These are tenderness-without-sentimentality territory.
- **The implant connection should be played, not spelled.** Do not have a character explain that voices may be implants or drugs or doctrine. Let the reader hold all three possibilities at once and not resolve them.
