# Intercontinental Trade and Communication — Post-Collapse

## Can Anyone Import Anything from China at 12 Years?

**Effectively no.** Not at scale, not reliably, and not with anything resembling modern logistics.

## Why Container Shipping Is Dead

Modern container shipping requires a chain of dependencies, all of which break simultaneously:

| Dependency | Status at 12 Years |
|---|---|
| Heavy fuel oil / diesel | Requires functioning refineries, oil extraction, and distribution. All collapsed. |
| Functioning ports | Cranes need power, channels need dredging, docks need maintenance. Major ports are ruins. |
| GPS navigation | Satellites designed for 7.5–15 years. Without ground control, accuracy degrades within 6 months. At 12 years, most GPS satellites are non-functional or severely degraded. |
| Financial/insurance systems | Nonexistent. No letters of credit, no cargo insurance, no currency exchange. |
| Skilled crews | Possible in theory but scattered, with no recruitment pipeline. |
| Communication | Ship-to-shore radio possible but no port traffic control, no weather routing, no coordination. |
| Security | Piracy becomes the norm; no coast guards, no naval patrols on commercial lanes. |

## Key Chokepoints

### Suez Canal
- Requires constant dredging — desert sand fills the channel
- Without maintenance, probably silted enough to block large vessels within 3-5 years
- Small, shallow-draft vessels might still pass but navigation aids are gone
- The canal zone itself may be contested or irradiated depending on the war's reach

### Panama Canal
- Locks require electrical power to operate — without it, non-functional
- Gatun Lake water levels need active management
- Lock gates, seals, and mechanisms degrade without maintenance
- Effectively impassable at 12 years without major engineering effort

### Strait of Malacca
- Naturally passable (no locks) but shallow in places (25 metres in parts)
- Navigation aids gone; GPS dead; the strait is full of wrecked/abandoned vessels
- Piracy corridor

### Strait of Hormuz
- If the Iran war is the origin point of the collapse, this strait may be contaminated, mined, or blockaded by wreckage
- Naturally passable but politically the most dangerous waterway on Earth even before the collapse

## GPS Collapse Timeline

- GPS satellites designed for 7.5–15 years per unit
- Without ground control stations, accuracy degrades within 6 months
- After a few years, satellites fail individually as clocks drift and systems break
- At 12 years, the GPS constellation is mostly dead
- Celestial navigation (sextant, star tables) works but is a skill very few living people possess
- Some military/specialist groups may maintain partial satellite awareness but cannot rely on it

## What CAN Cross Oceans

### Sailing Vessels
- Humans sailed from China to East Africa (and back) centuries before engines existed
- Small, self-sufficient crews using celestial navigation could theoretically make intercontinental voyages
- China-to-Ireland sailing route (no Suez): ~20,000-25,000 km via the Cape of Good Hope. 3-6 months at sea.
- This would be extremely rare, dangerous, and carry tiny volumes (a few tonnes at most)
- Requires: a seaworthy vessel, skilled crew, celestial navigation knowledge, provisioning for months, and luck

### Relay Chains
- Goods passed community to community along coastal routes
- Enormously slow — months to years for China to Ireland
- Only viable for high-value, low-volume items (data, medicine, specialised components)
- Each handoff point introduces risk of loss, theft, or corruption

### Shortwave / HF Radio
- **This is the realistic intercontinental link.**
- Shortwave radio bounces signals off the ionosphere; it can reach globally with modest equipment
- A competent operator with a ~100-watt transmitter and a decent antenna can communicate across continents
- Equipment can be built or maintained from salvaged components
- Does not require satellites, internet, or any infrastructure beyond the radio itself
- Morse code works when voice doesn't (lower bandwidth, more reliable at distance)
- This is almost certainly how the Irish Catholic network communicates with the Wuyishan collective

## What This Means for the Story

### The Ireland-China Connection Is Informational, Not Material

- The Irish Catholic network and the Wuyishan collective communicate by radio (probably HF/shortwave)
- They exchange: doctrine, intelligence, moral guidance, news, coded messages
- They do NOT exchange: physical goods, supplies, people, or equipment in any routine way
- A single physical object carried from China to Ireland (or vice versa) would be extraordinary — the result of a months-long voyage by a rare, skilled crew. It would be an event in the story, not background logistics.

### The Collectives Are Materially Self-Sufficient

- The Wuyishan collective and its mesh network produce their own food, maintain their own equipment, and rely on regional (not global) supply chains
- High-tech components (drone parts, robot parts, AI hardware) are either salvaged from pre-collapse stocks, manufactured locally at low quality, or distributed within the regional mesh network
- There is no intercontinental supply line for these items
- This means the AI hardware running the doctrine system is finite and irreplaceable — a critical vulnerability

### Physical Trade That Might Exist

- **Coastal relay trade along Asia**: Chinese coastal communities trading with Southeast Asia, the Indian subcontinent, possibly East Africa. Small vessels, short hops, local knowledge.
- **Indian Ocean sailing routes**: If maritime communities survive around the Indian Ocean rim (Oman, East Africa, India, Sri Lanka, Indonesia), traditional sailing routes could partially revive. These existed for millennia pre-industrially.
- **The Silk Road overland**: Possible for high-value goods but passes through Central Asia, which may be heavily contaminated or conflict-affected from the war. Extremely slow and dangerous.
- **European coastal trade**: Small vessels moving between surviving communities on the Atlantic coast (Portugal, Spain, Brittany, Ireland). More plausible than intercontinental routes.

### Communication Hierarchy

| Method | Range | Speed | Volume | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HF/Shortwave radio | Global | Instant | Voice/Morse only | Moderate (atmospheric conditions) |
| Drone courier | Regional (~hundreds of km) | Hours-days | Physical data + small packages | Good within network |
| Relay chain (physical) | Intercontinental | Months-years | Small volumes | Very low |
| Sailing vessel | Intercontinental | Months | Tonnes (small) | Very low |
| Runners/walkers | Local-regional | Days-weeks | Messages only | Moderate |
