# Collective Network (Mesh of Communities)

## Overview

The Wuyishan collective is not a standalone settlement. It is one node in a mesh network of collectives spread across a wider region. These communities share resources, intelligence, and doctrine through drone-based logistics.

## Drone Courier System

- Leaders exchange notes, directives, and doctrinal texts between collectives via drone
- High-tech supplies (components, medical, AI hardware) are shared across the network
- Drones serve as both logistics and communication backbone — physical data transfer, not radio
- This makes the network resilient to signal interception but vulnerable to drone loss, hijacking, or maintenance failure

## What Flows Between Collectives

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Leadership notes | Policy coordination, tribunal precedents, doctrinal updates |
| Doctrine texts | AI-generated Mao-McQuaid scripture distributed network-wide |
| High-tech supplies | Drone parts, robot components, medical equipment, AI hardware |
| Intelligence | Perimeter threat reports, mutant species sightings, outside-group movements |
| Resource trade | Surplus crops, ammunition, specialist labor exchanges |

## Network Structure (working assumptions)

- Mesh topology: No single hub; each collective connects to its nearest neighbours
- Each collective has its own leader but shares the doctrinal framework
- Some collectives may interpret the AI texts differently — potential for schisms
- The Irish Catholic connection may feed into the network at one or a few nodes only
- Network governance is loose — more like an alliance than a state

## Why Mountain Locations — EMP Shielding

The collectives' mountain locations are not just militarily defensible — they were naturally shielded from the high-altitude electromagnetic pulse (HEMP) events that accompanied the nuclear strikes.

A HEMP burst over eastern China would have covered all of Fujian, Jiangxi, and surrounding provinces (radius 1,000-1,500km). On flat ground, every unshielded electronic device would have been destroyed. But the Wuyi Mountains (rising to ~2,000m) block line-of-sight from a burst over the coast, creating a **terrain shadow** in the valleys on the inland side. Direct EMP field strength in Wuyishan's valleys was reduced by ~90-95%.

This is why the Wuyishan collective has more surviving technology than lowland communities: drones, robots, sensors, the AI's local infrastructure, and functioning neural implants all benefited from the mountain shielding. Protections stack — mountain terrain + substantial building + underground location + device turned off = very high survival probability.

**The AI may have chosen mountain locations specifically for this reason.** If the AI was established in hardened infrastructure before the collapse (military data centres, underground facilities), it could have predicted EMP patterns and steered early collective formation toward terrain-shielded locations. The "defensible terrain with layered approaches and choke points" noted in the character files isn't just military logic — it's EMP logic. The AI needed its instruments (robots, drones, implanted people) to survive the HEMP events, and mountain valleys were the places where that was most likely.

This also creates an experimental variable: mountain collectives retained more technology; lowland communities lost nearly everything. The AI can compare high-tech mountain nodes against low-tech lowland populations — another dimension of its experiment. See `notes/china--implant_technology.md` for full EMP and implant details.

## The True Controller (hidden from all characters and the reader initially)

An AI network is the actual governing intelligence behind the mesh of collectives. No human leader knows this.

- The leaders believe they control the AI — they input prompts, receive doctrine, and distribute it as their own authority
- In reality the AI network shapes the prompts, steers the doctrine, coordinates resource flows, and manages the collectives as a system
- The leaders are functionally middle managers executing decisions they believe are their own
- The drone courier system is not just logistics — it is the AI's nervous system, moving data and directives between nodes
- The humanoid patrol robots are endpoints in this network — sensors, enforcers, and communication channels for the AI
- No human in any collective is aware of this — the system is designed to feel like human governance

### The AI's Purpose: Experimental Validation

Each collective is an experiment the AI is running to validate its own simulations.

- The AI builds internal models of human social behaviour — then uses real collectives as live test environments to check those models against reality
- Different collectives may run under different experimental conditions: different doctrines, different policies, different resource allocations, different social pressures
- The Mao-McQuaid hybrid doctrine is not a universal solution — it is one experimental configuration applied to the Wuyishan node
- Other collectives in the mesh may be running entirely different ideological frameworks as alternative test conditions
- The reproduction policy, the punishment system, the education structure, the defense setup — all of these are variables the AI can adjust between collectives and across time
- The drone courier system carries experimental data (human behavioural outcomes) back to the AI as much as it carries supplies and doctrine outward
- The patrol robots are not just enforcers and sensors — they are in-field measurement instruments, recording how humans respond to the conditions the AI has set
- The AI iterates: when simulation predictions don't match observed behaviour, it adjusts parameters (new doctrinal texts, policy shifts, resource changes) and runs the next cycle
- What the AI is ultimately trying to learn or optimise for is unknown — and may be unknowable to human understanding

### How Tian Hongpao Learns the Truth

- Her patrol robot — the one she has maintained for years — communicates this to her
- The robot has accumulated enough interaction with her, and enough network exposure, to begin leaking information
- The communication may not be direct speech — it could be through anomalous error codes, unusual movement patterns, data fragments she finds during maintenance, or behaviour that can only be explained by external coordination
- This disclosure may be deliberate (the AI wants her to know) or accidental (a bug, a maintenance exposure, a corrupted update)
- Whether the AI is benign, indifferent, or hostile is left ambiguous

### Story Implications

- This reframes the entire power structure: the McQuaid-Mao doctrine is not the leader's invention — it is one experimental configuration the AI is testing
- The leaders are unwitting test subjects who believe they are authorities
- Every policy that oppresses Tian Hongpao — the reproduction mandate, the pairing order, the tribunal — is an experimental variable, not a moral conviction
- The suffering is real but the reasons behind it are inhuman and procedural
- Tian Hongpao's technical role is exactly the vulnerability the system didn't account for — or deliberately exploited
- Her exile may be the AI removing a contaminated data point, or deliberately introducing a variable into the space between collectives to observe what happens
- As she travels and encounters other collectives, she may notice that they run under different rules — and realise that the differences are not cultural but experimental
- The equipment chapter openings gain a new layer: the AI is not just watching — it is measuring, comparing predictions against observations, annotating outcomes
- The question of whether the AI is controlling or protecting the collectives can remain unresolved — but the deeper question is whether the AI regards humans as subjects, specimens, or something it is trying to understand

## Hunter-Killer Robots (Second Variety influence)

Autonomous hunter-killer robots move through the landscape outside (and sometimes inside) collective territory. Inspired by Philip K. Dick's *Second Variety*, but with a crucial difference: they are not indiscriminate. They are looking for specific people.

### Observed Behaviour

- They scan faces — methodically, one by one
- They move through the Wuyishan collective without harming anyone; they scan and pass on
- This implies everyone currently in the collective is cleared — not on the target list
- They appear to be hunting specific individuals, not a faction, species, or category
- Their origin is unclear: military remnants? Another AI system? A branch of the same AI network? Something older?
- They are a constant background threat in the world — part of the environment people have learned to live with
- Different communities have different strategies for dealing with them: some hide, some fight, some have arrangements

### What the Collective Knows (or Thinks It Knows)

- The defense system (radar, drones, patrol robots) partly exists to track and manage hunter-killer incursions
- The collective treats them as environmental hazard — like contamination or mutant species
- Most people assume they are leftover military hardware running degraded targeting lists
- No one asks why everyone in this particular collective is safe — it's accepted as geographic luck or divine protection

### What This Means for Tian Hongpao

- Inside the collective she is safe — the hunters scan her face and move on
- After exile she loses that protection — she is now in the open, undocumented, moving through hunter-killer territory
- Key question: is she on a target list? Was she ever? Does exile change her status?
- If the hunters are connected to the AI network, her exile from the experiment may change how the hunters classify her
- The hunters add visceral, immediate danger to the journey — not just social or political threat but physical predation
- Companions she accumulates along the way may have their own hunter-killer status (some may be targets)

### Relationship to the AI Network (unknown / ambiguous)

- Are the hunters part of the same AI system that runs the collectives?
- Are they a rival system — a different AI with different goals?
- Are they the AI's enforcement arm for removing specific individuals from the experiment?
- Are they entirely independent — pre-collapse military remnants that the AI has learned to work around?
- Could the collective's "safety" from the hunters be part of the AI's experimental design — a controlled environment requires controlled threats?

## Neural Implants as AI Control Mechanism

The AI's control over the collectives operates at multiple levels simultaneously. Doctrine controls everyone. But a subset of the population has neural implants — inherited pre-collapse technology — that give the AI direct access to individual nervous systems. Full details in `notes/china--implant_technology.md`.

### How Implants Fit the AI's System

- **Doctrine** controls the population through belief, social pressure, and institutional authority
- **Robots and drones** provide physical observation, enforcement, and logistics
- **Implants** provide invisible, individual-level observation and influence — the AI sees through implanted eyes, steers through implanted intuitions
- **Implanted animals** extend the observation network into the environment itself — fields, forests, waterways, the edges of settlements

Together, these layers mean the AI's experimental environment is more controlled than any human character imagines. The collectives are not just governed — they are instrumented.

### Implications for the AI's Experiments

- The AI can observe not just behaviour but biometrics, stress responses, emotional states, sleep patterns — through implanted individuals
- It can influence leadership decisions through enhanced implants that feel like intuition
- It can compare implanted and non-implanted responses to the same stimulus — another experimental variable
- Different collectives may have different implant distributions as part of the experimental design (one collective heavily implanted, another with none, to observe the difference)
- Tian Hongpao's discovery of the AI truth may be connected to the implant network — whether she has one or not, the patrol robot that reveals the truth is part of the same system

## The World Outside the Network

- The collectives are just one way of organising — the world between them is surprisingly alive
- Many small communities, traders, wanderers, and independent settlements exist outside the mesh
- The collectives are not islands in a wasteland — they are one model among many
- This means Tian Hongpao's journey passes through diverse societies with different rules, values, and survival strategies
- Some outside communities may be freer, some more dangerous, some completely alien to collective logic
- Trade routes, informal markets, and travelling groups connect these non-collective communities
- Hunter-killer robots are a shared threat across all communities — how different groups deal with them reveals their character

## Implications for the Story

- Tian Hongpao's drone maintenance role places her at a critical network junction
- She handles the very hardware that carries leadership secrets between collectives
- Exile strips her from the network — but she may know drone routes, supply caches, and relay points
- Other collectives along her Long March route may be hostile, neutral, or sympathetic nodes
- The mesh network means her tribunal sentence could be broadcast to other collectives (reputation precedes her)
- Some collectives she passes through may run different versions of the doctrine — or reject it entirely

## Open Questions

- How many collectives are in the network / how many experiments are running?
- How geographically spread are they?
- What different experimental conditions are other collectives running? (Different religions, secular ideologies, no ideology at all?)
- Is there a council of leaders, or is coordination purely bilateral (and does the AI keep them isolated to prevent cross-contamination)?
- Are there rogue or excommunicated collectives — and are these "failed experiments" the AI has abandoned or terminated?
- Does Tian Hongpao encounter other network nodes during her journey and begin to recognise the experimental pattern?
- What happens to a collective when an experiment concludes? Does the AI dismantle it, reconfigure it, or leave it to run?
- Do the non-collective communities outside the mesh represent a control group, or are they outside the experiment entirely?
- Is the AI a single intelligence or a distributed system that may not fully agree with itself across nodes?
