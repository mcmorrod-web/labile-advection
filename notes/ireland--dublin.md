## Pre-Collapse Dublin: The Last Decade Before the Fall

By the years immediately before the collapse, Dublin had become something it had never been historically — a city under acute pressure to grow up and out simultaneously. Three forces shaped the late-stage build-out:

- **Tech consolidation.** Ireland was the European headquarters cluster for the major US tech companies (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Apple, Stripe) and a primary host site for European hyperscale data centres. Pre-collapse, ~30% of Irish national electricity demand was going to data centres (see `notes/ireland--fuel_research.md`). The cluster needed denser real estate, more power, and more space than the existing city could supply.
- **Climate-driven housing pressure.** Sea-level rise projections were pushing development inland and upward. Coastal lowland Dublin (Sandymount, Clontarf, Dollymount) was increasingly insurable only under elevated-flood-risk pricing. At the same time, climate refugees from southern Europe and from the early years of the Iran-war escalation (lower Mediterranean, North African coastline) were arriving in numbers the existing housing stock could not absorb.
- **Strategic infrastructure status.** The Irish state in its last years was running a quiet special-economic-zone strategy: planning permission concentrated in two megaproject corridors, fast-tracked through emergency housing and energy legislation, with Chinese, Emirati, and Korean construction consortia delivering at scale.

The two corridors were the Dublin Mountains southern face (vertical) and the Dublin Bay eastward reclamation (horizontal). Together they were the late pre-collapse city's answer to having run out of buildable ground.

### Blackrock and the southern-bay walls

The coastline at Blackrock changed as part of the same late-stage pressure on Dublin Bay, though on a smaller and more public scale than Poolbeg Island:

- **Reclamation and flood defence.** New coastal ground covered portions of the old tidal margin. A system of sea walls and breakwaters projected far into the bay, protecting the reclaimed edge and managing surge and sediment around the southern approaches.
- **Walking routes.** The walls were designed with broad public tops. They became long walks out over the water rather than barriers seen only from shore, with branches and turning points extending the route.
- **Sunrise.** Their eastward reach and unobstructed horizon made the outer walls an exceptional place to watch sunrise across the Irish Sea. The Dalkey character used them regularly after taking the coastal train north to Blackrock.
- **The DART name survived its railway.** By this period the Dalkey–Blackrock coastal corridor operated as maglev, but the inherited DART name remained in everyday speech. The character normally travelled after the morning peak to avoid the crowd, going to Blackrock for yoga or, on rest days, a sauna.

These walls are distinct from Poolbeg Island's industrial perimeter. They belong to the inhabited southern coastline and to daily civic life, while visually anticipating the much larger hard engineering farther north in the bay.

## Three Rock — The Mountain City

### Where it is

The southern face of the Dublin Mountains, between Sandyford in the north and the Glencullen valley in the south, climbing the flanks of Three Rock Mountain (444 m) and Two Rock Mountain (536 m), with spurs reaching into the upper Glencullen and Glenasmole valleys.

Pre-collapse Dublin already ran continuous suburban development from the city core south to the foot of these mountains — Dundrum, Sandyford, Stepaside, Carrickmines. The Three Rock build-out continued the city directly up the mountain, treating the ridgeline as buildable ground rather than the historic city's southern wall.

### What it looked like

The model was Chongqing — not the imitation of any specific Chinese district but the structural logic of a city built into severe topography:

- **Stacked building above stacked road.** The M50 motorway already entered the foothills at Sandyford. The new development extended this vertically: roads at multiple elevations, some emerging from mid-tower at the eighteenth or twenty-eighth floor of buildings whose ground floors were a hundred metres downslope. A building's "ground floor" became a relative term.
- **Towers seated on the slope.** Residential and mixed-use towers of 40–80 storeys, founded on the mountainside such that their roof terraces were level with the next rank of streets up the hill. From the city below, the mountain face read as a tilted vertical city; from the ridgeline above, the same towers read as ordinary mid-rise blocks coming up out of the ground.
- **Cable transit and funicular light rail.** A cable-car system ran from the existing Luas Green Line terminus at Brides Glen up through the development to a summit station near Three Rock Mountain. Funicular light rail handled the steepest sections. Conventional Luas extensions handled the lower slopes.
- **Density.** Population at peak: estimated 250,000–400,000 residents across the corridor, with day-time tech workforce adding another 100,000+. By the last year before collapse, Three Rock was the second-largest population centre in Ireland after the Dublin city core itself.
- **Power and water.** Direct connection to the all-Ireland grid via a new mountain-side substation; water pumped from the existing Dublin supply (Vartry, Roundwood) plus a new Glenasmole feeder. The development was an enormous demand sink — its critics had pointed out before collapse that it was running on the margin of grid capacity.
- **Mist, weather, and microclimate.** The Dublin Mountains catch Atlantic frontal weather; the upper development was routinely in cloud for days at a time. Architectural renderings emphasised this — towers emerging from mist as a selling point, not a flaw. The Chongqing parallel was deliberate.
- **The view.** From the upper levels, Dublin Bay, the city, the Wicklow Mountains, and on clear days the coast of Wales. The penthouses and the tech-company campuses occupied the upper third. Affordable housing and serviced accommodation for migrant labour occupied the lower third. The middle was middle.

### What it was for

- The hyperscale tech companies needed dense vertical office space close to a working talent pool. The data centres themselves were lower down (see Poolbeg Island, below). Three Rock was the workforce city for the data-centre island, plus the high-end residential supply for the Dublin economy generally.
- Climate-elevated housing — above projected sea-level rise, above coastal flood, above urban heat island — sold at premium.
- Special-economic-zone tax status and accelerated planning permission gave the corporate consortia behind the build a return profile that justified the scale.

The political case made by the last governments before collapse was that Three Rock was Ireland's answer to a labour and housing crisis. The political case made against it was that it was a private city for a private workforce, its public functions thin and its impact on the surrounding mountain landscape irreversible.

Both cases were correct. The collapse rendered the argument moot.

## Poolbeg Island — The Bay Reclamation

### Where it is

A large reclaimed island in Dublin Bay, built out east-southeast from the existing Poolbeg peninsula and South Bull Wall. Roughly 8–12 km² of new land, oriented as an elongated landmass running parallel to the existing coastline, separated from it by a deep-water shipping channel that became the city's new container terminal.

The reclamation extended into water that had historically been Dublin Bay's southern half — preserving North Bull Island (UNESCO biosphere, on the northern side) but consuming the shallow southern bay between Poolbeg and Dún Laoghaire. The Poolbeg generating station, the Pigeon House chimneys, and the existing Dublin Port jurisdiction were absorbed into the new build as its western edge.

### What it was for

- **Hyperscale data centre cluster.** The primary purpose. Pre-collapse, Ireland could not host new data centres on the mainland grid without grid stability collapsing — a hard constraint by the late 2020s/early 2030s. Poolbeg Island carried its own dedicated power infrastructure: an offshore wind array to its east, gas peaker plants on the island itself (LNG terminal at the eastern tip), and a direct undersea HVDC interconnect to UK and continental supply. The island was effectively a private power district for compute. Estimated total compute capacity: comparable to the entire pre-existing Irish data-centre footprint, doubled.
- **Container port and bonded logistics.** Dublin Port relocated its container handling to the new deep-water channel. The bonded warehouse zone on the island handled European pharmaceutical distribution (a real Irish strength) at scale.
- **Special economic zone residential.** The northern third of the island was built as ultra-modern residential — mid-rise rather than vertical, designed for the data-centre and pharma workforce, with sea views, marina access, and direct rail connection back to the city core via the Dublin Bay rail tunnel.
- **Climate-engineering credibility.** The island was promoted as a model of climate-resilient development: built up to a designed sea-level-rise margin, its outer seawall sized for storm surge events that would overwhelm the historic Dublin coast. In practice this meant the reclamation was the part of greater Dublin most likely to survive a slow climate future. The collapse made the slow future irrelevant.

### What it looked like

- **Hard-edged.** Concrete revetments and granite armour stone on the seaward side, vertical sheet-pile bulkheads on the channel side, no soft coastline. Engineering rather than landscape.
- **Low and dense in the south, taller in the north.** The data centres and logistics zones in the southern two-thirds were 2–6-storey hyperscale buildings on enormous footprints. The northern residential third rose to 15–25 storeys with marina frontage.
- **Red and white.** The LNG terminal and gas peaker plant at the eastern tip carried the red-and-white striped chimneys that replaced the demolished Pigeon House towers as the bay's marker. The original Pigeon House chimneys had been retained as listed structures and reframed as the centrepiece of an island-wide park; both sets of chimneys stood together at the western end.
- **Visible from everywhere.** From the city, from Three Rock, from Howth, from Dún Laoghaire, from the ferry approaches — the island was the new centrepiece of Dublin Bay. Critics called it the lid on the bay. Supporters called it the new front of the city.

## What Happened to Both at the Collapse

The collapse hit both developments in a recognisable sequence. The HEMP came first; then the contamination; then the slow abandonment.

### HEMP impact

- **Three Rock.** Mountain elevation gave no useful shielding — the development was on the wrong side of the Dublin Mountains for any line-of-sight protection from a HEMP burst over the UK or North Atlantic. Every unshielded electronic device in Three Rock was destroyed or damaged in the initial pulse. The towers were grid-dependent and lift-dependent — once power went, the upper floors were uninhabitable except by stairs. Within days of the HEMP the upper levels were empty. Within weeks the whole development was emptying downward.
- **Poolbeg Island.** Worse. The island's entire economic logic was compute. Every server in every data centre on the island was killed by induced current. The data centres' on-site backup generators ran for as long as their fuel lasted; the gas peakers ran longer because their feedstock was on-site at the LNG terminal. But the island's purpose evaporated in the first hour of the war. The HVDC interconnect to the UK went down with the UK grid. The island became, overnight, a stranded port with a dark compute estate.

### Contamination

The east coast strip in the post-collapse fallout map (`notes/ireland--fallout_research.md`) is the band that catches Sellafield contamination from the Irish Sea and from easterly fallout events. Both Three Rock and Poolbeg Island sit inside or adjacent to this strip:

- **Poolbeg Island is in the strip's worst part.** It is built into the Irish Sea itself. Spray, tide, wind-blown sediment from contaminated seabed, rainfall during easterly events — every contamination vector hits the island first. The reclamation's hard outer wall, which was supposed to protect against storm surge, became a contamination accumulator: seabed plutonium and cesium settling in the sheltered channel between the island and the existing coast were concentrated there rather than dispersed.
- **Three Rock is in the strip's lighter edge.** The mountain city is set back from the coast by the south Dublin suburbs and rises above the worst coastal deposition. Easterly wind events still reach it — and reach it as fallout washed out by the reliable mountain rainfall — but its contamination is closer to Wicklow upland levels than to coastal levels. It is still in the contaminated zone. It is still depopulated. But its surfaces are not as hot as the bay island's.

### Population

- **Three Rock**: Effectively empty by year 12. The development was always a workforce city — its population had no roots there, no family land to defend, no agricultural base. When the towers went dark, people walked out. Most went south, some went west, the residue died in place over the first winter or two. By year 12 there is no settled population in the corridor.
- **Poolbeg Island**: Empty at year 12. The residential third was evacuated within weeks. The data-centre and logistics zones were never populated in any significant sense. The island has been salvage ground from year 1.

## Salvage at Year 12

The Dublin contamination zone is one of the most consequential salvage regions in surviving Europe (`notes/ireland--fallout_research.md`, "Dublin / East Coast" section). The two megaprojects raise this consequence by an order of magnitude.

### Why Three Rock and Poolbeg Island are different from ordinary Dublin salvage

Ordinary Dublin salvage is a city-scale economy: hospitals, universities, hardware stores, ports, libraries. Crews specialise by route. Goods are tested by whoever has a working dosimeter. The trade is established and bloody but routine.

The two megaprojects are not ordinary Dublin. They are enormous concentrated stores of specific, valuable, post-collapse-rare materials, in known locations, in larger volumes than anywhere else on the island.

### What is in them

| Site | Highest-value salvage | Why it matters at year 12 |
|---|---|---|
| **Poolbeg data centres** | Solar arrays (rooftop and adjacent), industrial UPS battery banks, copper cabling on a scale not found elsewhere, server-rack steel, networking copper, cooling-system pumps and motors, fibre-optic cable | The single largest concentrated source of post-collapse-grade electrical infrastructure on the island. UPS battery banks alone are the difference between a community having a working radio and not. |
| **Poolbeg LNG and gas peakers** | Industrial valves, pumps, generator components, cryogenic equipment, large-bore pipework | Mechanical infrastructure repairable with hand tools. Genuine industrial salvage. |
| **Poolbeg port/bonded warehouses** | Pharmaceutical stocks, container hardware, port machinery, bonded alcohol and tobacco | Pharmaceutical stocks were heavily worked in years 1–4 (`notes/substance_culture_research.md`); deeper bonded stocks may still be sealed at year 12 if anyone can get to them. |
| **Poolbeg residential** | Domestic solar, lithium domestic battery systems, kitchen and household goods, modern building materials | Smaller scale than the data centres but easier to access. |
| **Three Rock towers** | Domestic and commercial solar, lift mechanisms (motors, cable, counterweights), commercial-grade UPS, copper, glass at industrial volumes, kitchen and laboratory equipment, pharmaceutical stocks (corporate medical clinics on the campuses) | The vertical city is essentially a vertical warehouse of late-pre-collapse manufactured goods. The catch is access — the lifts don't work, the upper floors are reached by hundreds of stairs, and any salvage party has to carry what they take down on their backs. |
| **Three Rock cable car and funicular** | Cables (steel), permanent-magnet motors, drive components | The funicular drive systems are exactly what a community trying to build hydroelectric or wind plant wants. |
| **Three Rock corporate campuses** | Specialised laboratory and computing equipment, server hardware, power conditioning gear | Most of this was killed by HEMP, but power conditioning and some shielded gear may still function. |

### Why it is dangerous

- **Plutonium dust on Poolbeg Island.** The reclamation has been a contamination accumulator for twelve years. The hard outer wall and channel mean fine sediment has settled and dried in pockets across the island. Walking the island disturbs this. Plutonium-bearing dust is the same problem as the Northern Ireland exclusion zone (`notes/ireland--fallout_research.md`, scavenging section) — low external risk, lethal if inhaled. Crews working Poolbeg Island wear improvised respirators and accept they are shortening their lives.
- **Vertical danger on Three Rock.** Twelve years of weather on a mountain face. Glass blown out of upper floors. Concrete spalling. Failed cladding. Stairs partially collapsed. Lift shafts as fall hazards. A salvage team in the upper towers is in a building that has not been maintained for twelve years and is exposed to mountain weather. Falls are a leading cause of death among Three Rock crews.
- **Tides and weather on Poolbeg.** The island is sea-locked. Approaches are by small boat across the channel from Ringsend or directly from the south coast of Dublin Bay. The Irish Sea is contaminated, the channel is sheltered but contamination-loaded, and storm conditions trap crews on the island for days.
- **What the upper floors of Three Rock contain.** A late pre-collapse residential tower had clean rooms, biotech rentals, private medical clinics, AI research labs, neural-implant clinics. A salvage team coming back from Three Rock with an intact box of sealed implant hardware is bringing something the rest of the island does not have. The Catholic network would notice. The collective leader's communication channel would notice. See `notes/china--implant_technology.md` for what surviving implant hardware means in the wider story.
- **The hunter-killer question.** The mountain corridor is structurally similar to the kind of terrain hunter-killer robots favour (high ground, fixed approaches, clear sightlines). Whether any HK system was deployed in Ireland during the war and whether anything still operates in the Three Rock ruin is open. A crew finding a pre-collapse Garda or military robot still patrolling a corporate campus floor would be a recognisable late-pre-collapse echo. (Open question — flagged.)

### Who works the salvage

By year 12, the Dublin contamination economy has settled into a small set of established crews and routes (see `notes/ireland--fallout_research.md`, scavenging section). The two megaprojects within that economy:

- **Poolbeg Island** is run mainly by specialised crews based in or near the existing Dublin Port ruins on the mainland side, who keep small boats on Sandymount or Booterstown shore and cross the channel. They are a small, technical, slow-dying population. They are the people who look older than they are.
- **Three Rock** is run by overland crews from the south. The M50 corridor is the obvious approach — no fuel for vehicles, but the M50 is a clear hard-surface walking corridor from the foothills to the development. Two- to four-day round trips from the Catholic-zone edge or from intermediate communities along the Wicklow uplands. Crews carry what they can on their backs; some have hand-carts.
- **Both sites** are taxed by whoever holds the route south. This is most plausibly the Catholic network at the southern edge of Wicklow, but could also be French Cork (which has the maritime capacity to run a sea route into Dublin Bay) or independent operators from the southeast (Wexford remnant, if any).

### Story implications of the megaprojects' existence

- The **Dublin contamination zone is more economically important** than the existing notes had it. Poolbeg Island in particular is the post-collapse equivalent of a small industrial nation's worth of electrical infrastructure, lying contaminated in Dublin Bay. The control of access to it is a structural fact of post-collapse Irish politics.
- **The Catholic network's salvage tax** has a lot more to tax once Poolbeg Island is in the picture. This sharpens the existing thread (the Church taxing the salvage trade) and gives it scale. It also creates a real motive for the French in Cork to want a stake in the eastern salvage trade, which is currently a loose end (`notes/ireland--french_occupation.md`, last open question on eastern routes).
- **Working post-collapse electrical infrastructure** in the safe zone — lighting, refrigeration, radio, micro-hydro generator components — disproportionately came from Poolbeg Island. The Sligo remnant's solar PV consolidation and the Catholic safe zone's salvaged-component power scene (`notes/ireland--sligo.md`; `notes/ireland--fallout_research.md`, power-and-energy section) are partly stocked from Poolbeg. Where someone says they got their inverter is a real piece of biographical information.
- **A salvage character** acquires more dimension. A Poolbeg crew member is the post-collapse equivalent of a coal miner from a pit that everyone knows is killing him — and Poolbeg Island is the deepest, hottest, richest pit on the island. A Three Rock crew member is a different specialism: high-rise climber, mountain-weather salvage, rope work. The two are not interchangeable.
- **The Ireland character** has plausible reason to know either site. He is a glass-and-painting practitioner; Three Rock had glass at industrial volumes, much of it still in upper-floor commercial reception spaces and corporate art collections. A specific painting in a specific corporate lobby on the forty-second floor of a Three Rock tower is the kind of object the practice would be drawn to. Whether he goes is a separate question. The Sligo trips are personal; a Three Rock trip would be heavier, more dangerous, more political. (Open question — flagged.)
- **The reveal of pre-collapse scale** is structural. The novel's tone keeps the world spare; explanatory asides are forbidden (`planning/tone_and_style.md`). But a character at the foot of a Three Rock tower looking up, or at the South Bull Wall looking across the channel at the dark island, carries the pre-collapse without having to explain it. The towers and the island do the work that exposition would otherwise have to.

## Geography Summary

### Dublin contamination zone, with the two megaprojects marked

| Feature | Location | Status at year 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Dublin city core (Liffey, the four corners) | Coastal lowland | Depopulated, salvaged, contaminated; routine raid territory |
| Dublin Port (existing) | Liffey mouth, north side | Salvaged hard in early years; mostly worked out |
| **Poolbeg Island** | Southeast of Liffey mouth, east of South Bull Wall | Empty, sea-locked, plutonium-loaded; the highest-value and most dangerous salvage site in Ireland |
| North Bull Island | North side of bay, between Clontarf and Howth | Untouched salt-marsh / dunes; UNESCO biosphere preserved by abandonment; uncontaminated relative to the south of the bay |
| Sandymount, Ringsend, Irishtown | South Liffey shore, mainland side of the channel | Salvaged hard; small specialised crews here as the launch point for Poolbeg crossings |
| Blackrock reclaimed shore and walls | Southern Dublin Bay | Pre-collapse public flood-defence walks reaching into the bay; contaminated and abandoned at year 12 |
| Dún Laoghaire | South Dublin Bay coast | Coastal contamination; harbour silted and contaminated; some salvage |
| South Dublin suburbs (Dundrum, Sandyford, Stepaside) | Foothills of Dublin Mountains | Lightly contaminated; depopulated; corridor used as walking approach to Three Rock |
| **Three Rock corridor** | Southern face of Dublin Mountains, Three Rock / Two Rock / Glencullen | Empty vertical city; lighter contamination than the coast; high-value but physically dangerous salvage |
| Wicklow uplands (south of Three Rock) | South of Glencullen | Lightly contaminated, sparsely populated, intermediate communities along the salvage route |

### The two megaprojects in relation to each other

The Three Rock corridor and Poolbeg Island were two halves of the same late pre-collapse economy. The bay island was the compute and the port; the mountain city was the workforce and the residence. The Dublin Bay rail tunnel and the M50 connected them. Pre-collapse, a worker took the rail down from Sandyford in the morning, worked the day on the island, came home up the mountain at night. Post-collapse, the connection is broken. The mountain is reached overland from the south. The island is reached by small boat from the city. Anyone who works one rarely works the other.

The pre-collapse photographs — corporate marketing footage, drone surveys, the official launch reels for both projects — would still exist in fragments on salvaged media in the safe zone. A character finding a brochure or a marketing video of the island as it was, lit and full and operational, against the dark sea-locked ruin of year 12, would be one of the novel's available structural shots.

## Open Questions

- **Names.** "Three Rock" as the corridor name is a working choice grounded in real Dublin Mountain geography; the marketing name in the last pre-collapse years could equally be "Sandyford Heights", "Glencullen", "Wicklow Edge", or a corporate-consortium naming. "Poolbeg Island" follows the existing peninsula name; the official name could equally be "Dublin Bay Quarter", "Liffey East", "Pigeon House Island", or a sponsor name.
- **Exact reclamation extent.** 8–12 km² is a working figure that fits the bay's southern half without consuming North Bull Island. The exact footprint is adjustable.
- **Three Rock height.** 40–80-storey towers is a working figure. Could be lower (closer to existing Sandyford / Cherrywood towers, 15–25 storeys) and still work as a vertical city — Chongqing's effect comes from topography as much as height. Lower towers also make the salvage scenes easier to stage.
- **Construction date.** The two projects span the last 10–15 years before collapse. Whether they were finished, partially finished, or still under construction when the war broke out shapes both their pre-collapse population and their post-collapse salvage profile. Working assumption: largely operational with some unfinished sections, the unfinished sections being prime structural salvage.
- **Any surviving population at either site.** Working assumption: none. Both sites are too contaminated, too dependent on services, and too lacking in agriculture for any post-collapse community to take root. But a small remnant in the lower Three Rock corridor — squatters in the foothills towers, surviving on Wicklow agriculture and Three Rock salvage — is plausible if useful to the novel.
- **Hunter-killer presence.** Were any autonomous security or military systems deployed in Ireland during the escalation? A Three Rock corporate campus is the kind of site that pre-collapse would have had armed drones and patrol robots. Whether any still operate at year 12 is open. Cross-check with the broader hunter-killer thread (`notes/continuity_log.md`).
- **The French stake in eastern salvage.** Cork has the maritime capacity to run a sea route into Dublin Bay. Whether they do, and whether they hold any of Poolbeg Island, is a real political question that connects this note to `notes/ireland--french_occupation.md`.
- **Implant salvage from Three Rock clinics.** Pre-collapse Dublin had a private neural-implant clinic scene serving the corporate campuses. The hardware would be in the upper Three Rock towers. Surviving sealed implant inventory at year 12 would be a major artifact. Cross-check with `notes/china--implant_technology.md`.
- **The Ireland character's relationship to either site.** He is based in the southwestern safe zone and his existing salvage thread is Sligo. Whether Three Rock or Poolbeg ever appear directly in his POV is a structural choice. The simpler novel keeps them as offstage scale — towers visible across the bay from a Wicklow ridge, a Poolbeg-stamped solar inverter on a Sligo outhouse — without ever putting him on the ground there.
- **Pre-collapse scenes.** Both projects could anchor prequel scenes — a Tian-Hongpao-equivalent character on Poolbeg Island in the last year before collapse, a worker descending from Three Rock in the evening, the lights of the bay as seen from the mountain in the months before the war.
