## 1. Ireland Fallout Map

**File:** `assets/ireland--fallout_map.png`

### What It Shows

The full island of Ireland divided into four contamination zones at ~12 years post-collapse, plus the contaminated Irish Sea and the three UK contamination sources.

### Zone Descriptions (north to south)

**Zone 1: Northern Ireland — Permanent Exclusion Zone (dark red)**
- Covers all six counties of Northern Ireland plus the border counties (Donegal, Leitrim, Cavan, Monaghan, Louth)
- Source: Sellafield plutonium (Pu-239, half-life 24,000 years) + Liverpool conventional fallout
- Key cities marked: Belfast, Derry — both abandoned
- This zone is qualitatively different from the others: the plutonium means it is not recoverable on any human timescale
- The boundary is not sharp — contamination grades southward into the east coast zone

**Zone 2: East Coast Strip (orange)**
- A band along the eastern seaboard from north Dublin south through Wicklow to the Wexford coast
- Width varies: roughly 20-50km inland from the coast
- Source: Irish Sea contamination from Sellafield waste entering the water, plus direct fallout from easterly wind events
- Dublin marked — partially evacuated, depopulated, patchy contamination depending on rainfall during fallout events
- Plutonium levels lower than Northern Ireland but present near the coast
- Coastal fishing: dead (Irish Sea contaminated)
- Some areas may be re-habitable in 30-50 years with monitoring

**Zone 3: Waterford-Limerick Band — Contamination Belt (yellow/amber)**
- A roughly east-west band across the middle-south of the island
- Eastern extent: Waterford (southeast coast)
- Western extent: Limerick / Shannon estuary (west coast)
- Width: roughly 30-60km north to south, following rising ground where fallout deposited
- Source: Hinkley Point (Somerset) reactor contamination, carried across the Celtic Sea under northerly/northeasterly winds
- Primarily Cesium-137 (half-life 30 years) — no significant plutonium
- At 12 years: NOT safe for farming. Cesium actively cycling in Ireland's wet peat soils.
- Some people live here (subsistence, no farming) but cannot safely grow food
- The band is not a wall — it grades from heavier contamination in the centre to lighter at the edges

**Zone 4: South of the Line — Safe Zone (green)**
- Everything below the Waterford-Limerick band
- Includes: Kerry, Cork, Clare (south of Shannon), southern Tipperary, southwest coast
- Clean: mild climate, agricultural base, Atlantic rainfall (clean westerly weather)
- This is where functioning Irish society exists
- Safe for farming, safe for habitation
- Population: ~500,000-800,000 Irish + ~510,000-520,000 in French-controlled Cork

### Sea and Sources

**Irish Sea (grey-blue)**
- The entire Irish Sea between Ireland and Britain is contaminated from Sellafield waste
- No fishing, no safe coastal activity on either side
- This shapes all trade and movement — east coast ports are useless

**Contamination Sources (marked on UK coast with arrows)**
- **Sellafield** (Cumbria, northwest England): ~140 tonnes of stored plutonium released. Arrow pointing west across the Irish Sea toward Belfast/Dublin
- **Liverpool**: Direct strike or catastrophic industrial contamination. Arrow pointing west toward Belfast/Dublin
- **Hinkley Point** (Somerset, southwest England): Reactor contamination. Arrow pointing southwest across the Celtic Sea toward the Waterford-Limerick band

### Notes for Accurate Redrawing

- The zone boundaries should follow real Irish geography: the contamination band roughly follows the line of higher ground between Waterford and Limerick (Comeragh Mountains, Galtee Mountains, Ballyhoura Hills)
- The east coast strip follows the coastline, tapering inland
- Northern Ireland's boundary is the real border plus an overspill into the Republic's border counties
- The zones grade into each other — no hard lines. Use gradient edges rather than sharp boundaries if possible.

## 1b. Northern Ireland Refugee Routes Map

**File:** `assets/ireland--northern_refugee_routes_map.png`

### What It Shows

The two emigration routes taken by Northern Ireland survivors in the first 1-2 years after the collapse: the short crossing to Scotland (the main route) and the long Atlantic route south to Ireland (the smaller, more desperate group that ends up in French-controlled Cork).

### Route 1: To Scotland (main route)

- **Launch points:** Torr Head, Ballycastle, Portrush — the north Antrim coast
- **Crossing:** ~20km across the North Channel to the Mull of Kintyre, Scotland
- **This is the route most NI survivors take.** It's a morning's sailing in calm weather, survivable even in marginal boats.
- Scotland (or parts of it) is habitable — the Highlands and west coast survived
- The NI survivors who cross to Scotland are a mix of Protestant and Catholic

### Route 2: South to Ireland (smaller group)

- **Launch points:** North coast ports (Portrush, Ballycastle) and western Donegal (already on the Atlantic side)
- **Route:** Around Malin Head (northernmost point of Ireland), then south down the entire Atlantic west coast
- **Distance:** 350–550km depending on destination. Multi-day journey in open Atlantic water. Genuinely dangerous.
- **Who takes this route:** Estimated 5,000–20,000 total over the first 1-2 years, then it stops. Disproportionately Protestant/Unionist (Catholics in NI died at higher rates from contamination geography; some surviving Catholics went to Scotland instead fearing exclusion by the southern Catholic power structure).
- **Why not the Irish Sea (east coast)?** Contaminated from Sellafield. The east coast route is effectively closed.

### West Coast Landings and Rejection

- Early arrivals land scattered along the west coast: Galway Bay, Clare coast, Kerry coast
- **Resisted by the Catholic network** — too many mouths, wrong denomination, outsiders in territory already squeezed between the contamination band and the French occupation
- Boats turned away, refugees denied land and food, told to move on
- Mark rejection points at Galway, Clare, and Kerry on the map

### Onward to Cork

- Word spreads: **the French in Cork will accept newcomers**
- Later boats bypass the west coast entirely and sail directly around the south coast to Cork Harbour
- The French don't care about the Protestant/Catholic divide — they need workers, especially people with maritime skills
- By 12 years: the northerners are integrated into French Cork — intermarriage, hybrid community

### Features to Mark

- **NI exclusion zone** (dark red): The entire north, source of the refugees
- **Contaminated Irish Sea** (grey): Explains why east coast route is closed
- **Scotland** (green, northeast): Short crossing destination, labelled as habitable
- **Route 1 arrow** (green, short): Torr Head → Mull of Kintyre, ~20km
- **Route 2 arrow** (orange, long dashed): North coast → Malin Head → south down Atlantic coast → west coast landings (rejected) → around to Cork Harbour
- **Rejection markers** (X symbols): At Galway, Clare, Kerry
- **Cork Harbour** (endpoint): French zone, accepts refugees
- **Launch points** labelled: Portrush, Ballycastle, Torr Head, western Donegal
- **Malin Head** labelled: The turning point where boats round the northwest corner

### Notes for Accurate Redrawing

- The Atlantic west coast of Ireland is one of the most exposed coastlines in Europe — massive swells, no shelter for long stretches
- The route around Malin Head is notorious even for experienced sailors
- Donegal is a secondary launch point — people from the western border region who were already on the Atlantic side
- The distance from Ballycastle to Galway by sea is ~400km; to Cork Harbour is ~700km
- Show the route hugging the coast (small boats stay in sight of land) rather than crossing open water

## 2. Ireland French Occupation Map

**File:** `assets/ireland--french_occupation_map.png`

### What It Shows

A zoomed-in view of the safe zone (southern Ireland), showing French-controlled territory, Irish-controlled territory, the disputed frontier, and the contamination band as northern context.

### Territory Descriptions

**French-Controlled Territory (~4,000 km², centred on Cork)**

Core zone:
- **Cork City**: French HQ and administrative centre. On the River Lee.
- **Cobh / Cork Harbour**: Port and naval base. One of the world's largest natural harbours. Controls sea access.
- **Midleton**: Eastern anchor. Agricultural hinterland east of Cork city.

South coast (coastal strip):
- **Kinsale**: Fishing port and secondary harbour, ~25km south of Cork
- **Bandon**: Market town, river crossing, ~30km southwest of Cork
- **Clonakilty**: Agricultural centre, ~50km southwest of Cork
- **Skibbereen**: Western extent of solid French control, ~80km southwest of Cork

Northern reach (inland):
- **Mallow**: Controls the Blackwater Valley. Northern frontier, ~35km north of Cork
- **Fermoy**: Eastern Blackwater. Frontier garrison, ~35km northeast of Cork

The Blackwater River runs roughly east-west and forms the natural northern defensive boundary of French territory.

**Disputed / Frontier Zone**
- A band along the Blackwater River and the uplands between Cork and Kerry
- Patrols, skirmishes, raids, uneasy local arrangements
- Some Irish communities within French territory remain but under French authority
- This is not a clean border — it's a contested zone with mixed control

**Irish-Controlled Territory**
- **Kerry**: Tralee, Killarney — likely the new centre of Irish Catholic network power. West of the French zone.
- **Clare** (south of Shannon): Ennis, the Burren. Northwest, above the Shannon estuary.
- **South Tipperary**: Reduced territory, squeezed between the contamination band to the north and French-adjacent areas to the south.
- **Western Cork peninsulas**: Beara, Sheep's Head, Mizen — may be disputed or Irish-held. These are the long fingers of land reaching southwest into the Atlantic.

**Contamination Band (northern context)**
- The Waterford-Limerick band shown as a shaded strip across the top of the map
- Shows that the safe zone is bounded: contamination to the north, Atlantic to the west and south, French occupation in the centre

### Key Geographic Features to Mark

- **Blackwater River**: The frontier line. Runs roughly east-west through Mallow and Fermoy.
- **River Lee**: Runs through Cork City to the harbour.
- **Cork Harbour**: The deep-water port that made Cork the French landing point.
- **Beara, Sheep's Head, Mizen peninsulas**: The western Cork fingers — disputed/Irish margin.
- **Dingle Peninsula**: Kerry, firmly Irish-controlled.
- **Shannon Estuary**: Northern boundary of Clare. Near the contamination band edge.

### Population Overlay (optional, could be shown as annotations)

| Group | Population | Territory |
|---|---|---|
| French (including ~5,000-15,000 northern Irish refugees) | ~510,000-520,000 | Cork zone |
| Irish (Catholic network) | ~500,000-800,000 | Kerry, Clare, margins |
| Contamination band settlers | Small, scattered | Waterford-Limerick band |

### Notes for Accurate Redrawing

- The French zone is roughly County Cork minus the western peninsulas
- The Blackwater River is the key boundary feature — find it on a real Cork map and trace it
- Kerry is separated from Cork by the Derrynasaggart and Caha mountain passes
- Clare is separated from Kerry/Limerick by the Shannon estuary
- The scale is small: Cork city to Killarney is only ~85km. The entire safe zone is roughly 150km × 100km.

## 3. Tian Hongpao's Journey Map

**File:** `assets/china--journey_map.png`

### What It Shows

The overland exile route from the Wuyishan collective (Fujian province) to Tungapuri (Kengtung/Kyaingtong, eastern Shan State, Myanmar). Approximately 2,000-2,500km across southern China and into Southeast Asia.

### Route (east to west)

**Start: Wuyishan, Fujian Province**
- Northwestern Fujian, on the border with Jiangxi province
- Mountainous terrain: the Wuyi Mountains rise to ~2,000m
- The collective occupies old tea plantation land in the mountain valleys
- Elevation: 200-800m in the valleys, surrounded by peaks

**Fujian → Jiangxi / Hunan border region**
- West from Wuyishan into Jiangxi province
- Crossing the mountainous spine that separates Fujian from inland China
- Terrain: forested mountains, river valleys, scattered settlements
- Distance: ~300-400km to clear the eastern mountain barrier

**Through Hunan or Guizhou**
- Two possible routes diverge here:
  - **Northern route**: Through southern Hunan (past Chenzhou, Hengyang area), then southwest into Guangxi or Guizhou
  - **Southern route**: Through northern Guangdong, then west into Guangxi
- Terrain: transitions from mountains to hills, river basins. The Xiang River (Hunan) or the Bei River (Guangdong) would be natural travel corridors
- Distance: ~500-700km across this middle section

**Guangxi or Guizhou → Yunnan**
- Continuing west, crossing into Yunnan province
- Terrain becomes increasingly mountainous again — the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau
- River valleys (the Yuan/Red River system, the Mekong system) run roughly north-south, cutting across the east-west travel direction
- This means repeated mountain climbs and descents crossing the grain of the land
- Key city/waypoint: Kunming area (if northern route) or Wenshan/Honghe area (if southern route)
- Distance: ~500-600km

**Yunnan → Xishuangbanna**
- South from the Yunnan plateau down to the tropical lowlands
- Dramatic elevation drop: from ~2,000m plateau to ~500-600m in Xishuangbanna
- The Mekong River (Lancang Jiang) runs through this section
- Jinghong is the main city of Xishuangbanna
- Terrain: transitions from temperate mountain forest to subtropical/tropical forest
- Distance: ~400-500km from Kunming area

**Xishuangbanna → Daluo Border Crossing**
- From Jinghong south to the Daluo border crossing on the Myanmar border
- Distance: ~110km
- The Daluo River marks the border

**Daluo → Tungapuri (Kengtung/Kyaingtong)**
- Cross from China into Myanmar's eastern Shan State
- ~80km through mountainous terrain
- Elevation: Tungapuri sits at 827m
- Mountain paths, no major roads in post-collapse conditions
- This is the territory the sibling knows — the old telecom fraud network routes

**End: Tungapuri (Kengtung/Kyaingtong), Eastern Shan State, Myanmar**
- Old Shan princely city, founded by Tai Lue people
- 827m elevation
- Crossroads city: Shan, Tai Lue, Chinese, Thai, Wa populations

### Terrain Summary

| Section | Terrain | Elevation | Approx Distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wuyishan → Jiangxi border | Mountains, forests | 200-2,000m | 300-400km |
| Jiangxi/Hunan crossing | Hills, river basins | 100-500m | 500-700km |
| Guangxi/Guizhou → Yunnan | Plateau, deep valleys | 500-2,000m | 500-600km |
| Yunnan → Xishuangbanna | Plateau to tropical lowland | 2,000m → 500m | 400-500km |
| Xishuangbanna → Daluo | Subtropical lowland | 500-600m | 110km |
| Daluo → Tungapuri | Mountain paths | 600-1,200m | 80km |
| **Total** | | | **~2,000-2,400km** |

### Features to Mark

- Provincial boundaries (Fujian, Jiangxi, Hunan/Guangdong, Guangxi/Guizhou, Yunnan)
- Major rivers crossed: Min River system, Xiang/Bei rivers, Yuan/Red River, Mekong (Lancang)
- China-Myanmar border
- Start point (Wuyishan) and end point (Tungapuri) prominently marked
- The route should look like a journey, not a straight line — it follows valleys, crosses passes, and avoids the highest terrain where possible

### Notes for Accurate Redrawing

- The route crosses the "grain" of southern China's geography: rivers and mountain ranges run roughly north-south, while the journey goes east-west. This means constant climbing and descending.
- The exact route is not yet determined — show it as a broad corridor rather than a precise line
- Multiple possible paths exist through the middle section (Hunan vs Guangdong, Guangxi vs Guizhou)
- Mark potential collective network nodes along the route (speculative — see collective network map)

## 4. Collective Network Map

**File:** `assets/china--collective_network_map.png`

### What It Shows

The mesh network of AI-controlled collectives, with Wuyishan as the known node and estimated/possible locations of other nodes across southern China and potentially Southeast Asia.

### Known Facts

- The Wuyishan collective (~50,000 people) is one node in a mesh network
- Mesh topology: no single hub; each collective connects to nearest neighbours
- Communication is via drone courier (physical data transfer, not radio)
- The AI controls the network; human leaders don't know this
- Different collectives may run different experimental conditions (different doctrines, policies, ideologies)

### Speculative Node Placement (working assumptions — flagged as open questions)

The network needs to be plausible given:
- Drone courier range (probably 100-300km per hop, depending on drone capability and terrain)
- Defensible terrain (collectives choose locations with natural barriers)
- Agricultural viability (each collective needs to feed its population)
- Post-collapse population distribution (concentrated in areas that survived the war)

**Possible node locations (all speculative, for discussion):**

| Node | Region | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Wuyishan** (confirmed) | NW Fujian | Tea country, mountain valleys, defensible |
| Somewhere in Jiangxi | Central/south Jiangxi | River valley agriculture, between Fujian and Hunan |
| Somewhere in Hunan | Southern Hunan | Rice country, Xiang River basin |
| Somewhere in Guizhou | Eastern Guizhou | Mountain plateau, remote, defensible |
| Somewhere in Guangxi | Northern Guangxi | Karst country, Li River area, agricultural |
| Somewhere in Yunnan | Central Yunnan (Kunming area?) | Plateau agriculture, mild climate |
| Somewhere in Guangdong | Northern Guangdong | If coastal cities destroyed, interior mountains viable |

**Spacing:** If nodes are roughly 200-400km apart, the network could span from Fujian to Yunnan with 5-8 nodes — enough to cover Tian Hongpao's journey route and provide encounters along the way.

### Open Questions (flagged for future worldbuilding)

- How many collectives total? (5? 10? 20?)
- Are they all in China, or do some extend into Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar?
- Do all nodes run Catholic-derived doctrine, or are some running entirely different experiments?
- Are there "dead" nodes — failed experiments the AI has abandoned?
- Does the network extend north of the Yangtze, or is it a southern China phenomenon?
- Is there a reason the AI chose this region? (Climate stability? Surviving infrastructure? Distance from worst contamination?)

### Notes for Accurate Redrawing

- Show southern China from Fujian to Myanmar
- Mark Wuyishan as the confirmed node (different symbol from speculative nodes)
- Show speculative nodes as question marks or dotted circles
- Draw drone courier range circles (~200-300km radius) around each node to show network connectivity
- Overlay Tian Hongpao's journey route to show which nodes she might pass through

## 5. Tungapuri / Border Region Map

**File:** `assets/china--tungapuri_border_map.png`

### What It Shows

A zoomed-in view of the Xishuangbanna (China) to Kengtung/Tungapuri (Myanmar) corridor — the sibling's territory and the endpoint of Tian Hongpao's journey.

### Geography

**Xishuangbanna (Chinese side)**
- **Jinghong**: Main city, on the Mekong River (called Lancang Jiang on the Chinese side). Elevation ~550m.
- Tropical/subtropical climate — very different from the rest of China
- Historically Tai Lue people (related to Thai, Lao)
- Pre-collapse: tourism, rubber plantations, tea, border trade
- The sibling ran a BnB here before the collapse

**Daluo Border Crossing**
- ~110km south of Jinghong
- On the Daluo River, which marks the China-Myanmar border
- Pre-collapse: a busy border town with formal crossing and many informal routes
- The telecom fraud networks used this crossing extensively
- Post-collapse: no state border control; controlled by whoever holds it locally

**Eastern Shan State (Myanmar side)**
- Mountainous terrain between the border and Kengtung
- ~80km as the crow flies, longer on mountain paths
- Elevation varies: 400-1,400m with multiple ridges and valleys
- Ethnic mix: Shan, Tai Lue, Akha, Lahu, Wa, Chinese
- Pre-collapse: opium/methamphetamine production zones, armed ethnic militias, Chinese telecom fraud compounds
- Post-collapse: the armed groups and smuggling networks became the governance structure

**Tungapuri (Kengtung/Kyaingtong)**
- Elevation: 827m, in a mountain valley
- Old Shan princely city, its own palace
- Founded by Tai Lue people, historically independent of Burmese central control
- Pre-collapse population: ~170,000 (Kengtung township)
- Crossroads position: routes to Thailand (south), Laos (east), China (north/northeast), rest of Shan State (west)
- The old telecom fraud infrastructure (safe houses, radios, supply caches, armed contacts) is now the communication and trade backbone

### Key Features to Mark

- **Mekong River**: Runs through Jinghong, continues south through Laos
- **Daluo River**: Border marker at the crossing point
- **Mountain ridges**: Between Daluo and Kengtung — at least 2-3 major ridge crossings
- **Jinghong**: Start of the border region
- **Daluo**: Border crossing
- **Tungapuri/Kengtung**: Destination
- **Routes to neighbouring countries**: South to Thailand (Mae Sai/Tachileik border), east to Laos, west deeper into Shan State
- **International borders**: China-Myanmar, and nearby Myanmar-Laos, Myanmar-Thailand (the Golden Triangle convergence)

### Notes for Accurate Redrawing

- This is the Golden Triangle area — where China, Myanmar, Laos, and Thailand nearly meet
- The Mekong is the dominant geographic feature
- The terrain is steep — even 80km can take days on foot through these mountains
- Show the informal path network (dotted lines) rather than just formal roads
- The scale is intimate compared to the journey map: the entire area shown is roughly 150km × 150km

## 6. Fujian Contamination / Mutation Zone Map

**File:** `assets/china--fujian_contamination_map.png`

### What It Shows

The area around the Wuyishan collective showing H-bomb contamination effects, the mutation zone, and hunter-killer robot patrol patterns.

### Known Facts

- An H-bomb was used during the escalation wars (referenced in collective backstory and world timeline)
- The Wuyishan region has a "post-disaster mutation zone" with "occasional novel species emerging from contamination effects"
- The collective chose this location partly for "defensible terrain with layered approaches and choke points" — but the mountains also provided critical EMP shielding (see below)
- Hunter-killer robots move through the landscape, including through the collective
- The collective has radar, drones, and humanoid patrol robots for defense

### Working Assumptions (flagged — these need confirmation/development)

**H-bomb Impact**
- The impact site is NOT Wuyishan itself (the collective exists there, so it wasn't ground zero)
- Likely a coastal city strike: Fuzhou (~250km east of Wuyishan) or somewhere on the Fujian coast
- The Wuyishan mountains may have partially shielded the valley from the worst blast effects
- Contamination would have drifted inland depending on wind patterns

**Mutation Zone**
- Extends outward from the impact/contamination area
- The Wuyishan collective is on the edge of this zone — close enough for novel species to appear, far enough to survive
- The mutation zone may follow river valleys (where contaminated water flows) and prevailing wind deposition patterns
- This creates a landscape where the terrain itself is unpredictable — familiar species alongside things that shouldn't exist

**Hunter-Killer Patrol Areas**
- The robots move through the wider landscape, not just the contamination zone
- They transit through the collective without attacking (everyone there is cleared)
- Their patrol routes may follow old roads, valleys, or logical movement corridors
- They are denser in some areas than others (but the pattern is unclear to the characters)

### Open Questions (flagged for future worldbuilding)

- Where exactly did the H-bomb hit? (Fuzhou? Xiamen? Somewhere else on the Fujian coast?)
- How large is the mutation zone? (10km radius? 50km? 100km?)
- Is the mutation zone expanding, stable, or contracting at 12 years?
- What is the contamination type — nuclear fallout, chemical, biological, or a combination?
- Are the hunter-killers more concentrated near the impact site, or do they range across a wider area?
- Is the collective's location a deliberate choice by the AI? (Edge of the mutation zone = interesting experimental conditions, AND the Wuyi Mountains provided ~90-95% EMP shielding from HEMP bursts over eastern China — preserving the AI's infrastructure, drones, robots, and functioning neural implants. See `notes/china--collective_network.md` and `notes/china--implant_technology.md`.)
- Are there other settlements within or near the mutation zone?

### Features to Mark

- **Wuyishan collective** (confirmed location): In the mountain valleys of northwestern Fujian
- **Estimated H-bomb impact site**: On or near the Fujian coast (speculative)
- **Mutation zone boundary**: Rough circle or irregular zone around the impact area, reaching inland to near Wuyishan
- **Wuyi Mountains**: The mountain range that shielded the collective — from blast effects (partial) AND from HEMP electromagnetic pulse (~90-95% field reduction in the terrain shadow). This is why the collective has more working technology than lowland communities.
- **Hunter-killer patrol routes**: Dotted lines or shaded areas showing movement corridors
- **Collective defense perimeter**: The radar/drone/patrol robot coverage area around the settlement
- **Nearby cities (ruins)**: Fuzhou, Nanping, Sanming — all likely destroyed or abandoned

### Notes for Accurate Redrawing

- Northwestern Fujian is genuinely mountainous — the Wuyi range forms the provincial border with Jiangxi
- The collective is likely in the valleys between 200-800m elevation, sheltered by peaks up to 2,000m
- The coast is ~250km east — the H-bomb blast effects would attenuate over that distance through mountain terrain. Critically, the 2,000m peaks block line-of-sight from a HEMP burst over the coast, creating a terrain shadow that reduced EMP field strength in the valleys by ~90-95%. This explains the collective's surviving technology base.
- River systems (Min River and tributaries) flow from the mountains toward the coast — contamination would follow these downstream, NOT upstream toward Wuyishan
- This means the collective is upstream from the worst contamination — a plausible reason to be relatively safe while still seeing edge effects (mutation zone species drifting upriver/uphill)
