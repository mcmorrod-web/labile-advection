# France and European Fallout Research

## France's Nuclear Infrastructure

- **56 operating reactors** across 18-19 sites
- **La Hague reprocessing plant** on the Cotentin Peninsula (Normandy coast): Holds half the world's light water reactor spent fuel reprocessing capacity. Over 32,000 tonnes of spent fuel processed. Stores separated plutonium, vitrified high-level waste, and intermediate waste. **This is France's Sellafield — and arguably worse.**
- Every reactor site is located on a major river (for cooling water): the Rhône, Loire, Seine, Garonne, Rhine, Meuse, Moselle
- Nuclear power produces ~65% of France's electricity — the most nuclear-dependent country on Earth

## If All Sites Are Hit: The Fallout Picture

### France Itself

France is essentially finished as a habitable country. With 18+ simultaneous contamination sources spread across the entire territory, there is no safe direction to flee.

**Possibly survivable pockets:**
- Deep Pyrenees valleys (far from any site, mountain barrier, Spanish side may be cleaner)
- Far southwestern Basque country (distance from nearest site + Atlantic weather)
- Corsica (island, ~170km offshore, depending on wind patterns during strikes)
- Parts of Brittany's western tip (depending on wind during La Hague / Gravelines events)
- None of these are guaranteed — they're just the least bad options

**Definitely uninhabitable:**
- Paris and Île-de-France (Nogent on the Seine + downwind from multiple sites)
- Rhône Valley from Lyon to Marseille (4 reactor sites in a line)
- Loire Valley (4 reactor sites)
- Northern coast (Gravelines + Penly + Paluel + Flamanville + La Hague)
- Northeast (Cattenom + Chooz, plus German/Belgian site fallout if those are also hit)

### Rivers Become Poison Highways

Every French reactor sits on a river. When destroyed, contaminated water enters the river system:

| River | Nuclear Sites | Flows To |
|---|---|---|
| Rhône | Bugey, Saint-Alban, Cruas, Tricastin | Mediterranean (near Marseille) |
| Loire | Chinon, Saint-Laurent, Dampierre, Belleville, Civaux | Atlantic (near Nantes) |
| Seine | Nogent | English Channel (near Le Havre) |
| Garonne/Gironde | Golfech, Blayais | Atlantic (near Bordeaux) |
| Moselle/Rhine | Cattenom | North Sea (via Netherlands) |
| Meuse | Chooz | North Sea (via Netherlands/Belgium) |

The rivers carry contamination hundreds of kilometres downstream and into the sea. The Mediterranean, the Atlantic coast, and the North Sea all receive contaminated river water.

### Impact on Neighbouring Countries

Prevailing European winds at temperate latitudes blow predominantly **west to east**. This means French fallout drifts toward:

**Germany:**
- Western Germany is catastrophically affected. Cattenom is practically on the border. Prevailing winds carry fallout from multiple French sites eastward across the Rhine.
- The Rhine itself carries contaminated water from Cattenom/Fessenheim area northward through Germany to the Netherlands
- If Germany's own reactors are also hit, the country is finished

**Belgium / Luxembourg / Netherlands:**
- Chooz is on the Belgian border. Cattenom is near Luxembourg. Gravelines is near the Belgian coast.
- Meuse and Rhine carry contaminated water through all three countries
- These small, densely populated countries have no distance buffer

**Switzerland:**
- Surrounded: French fallout from the west (Bugey is ~70km from Geneva), potentially Italian and German fallout from other directions
- Switzerland's famous civil defense infrastructure (shelter capacity for 114% of population) becomes genuinely relevant — this is one of the few countries where a significant portion of the population might survive underground
- But the surface is contaminated for decades

**Italy (Northwest):**
- Rhône Valley fallout (Bugey, Saint-Alban, Cruas, Tricastin) drifts toward the Alps and through passes
- Mediterranean contamination from Rhône outflow affects the Ligurian coast, Sardinia, potentially further
- Northern Italian plains (Po Valley) may receive fallout depending on wind patterns

**Spain (Northeast):**
- Pyrenees provide a partial barrier but not complete
- Golfech and Blayais are in the southwest; fallout can cross into Catalonia and Aragon
- Mediterranean coast affected by Rhône contamination

**UK (Southern Coast):**
- Northern French sites (Gravelines, Penly, Paluel, Flamanville, La Hague) are directly across the Channel
- Under southerly winds, fallout crosses to Kent, Sussex, Hampshire
- La Hague's plutonium + spent fuel is only ~130km from the English coast
- This ADDS to the Sellafield/Hinkley Point contamination already devastating the UK
- The English Channel becomes another dead waterway

### The Mediterranean

- The Rhône carries contamination from 4 reactor sites + Tricastin enrichment facility directly into the Mediterranean near Marseille
- The Mediterranean is semi-enclosed with slow water exchange through the Strait of Gibraltar (~100 years for full exchange)
- Contamination would persist in the Mediterranean far longer than in open ocean
- This affects: Southern France, Spain, Italy, Croatia, Greece, Turkey, North Africa, all Mediterranean islands
- Mediterranean fishing: Dead for generations

## La Hague — The Worst Single Site

La Hague deserves special attention because it is the single most dangerous nuclear site in Europe:

- Over 32,000 tonnes of spent fuel processed; ongoing storage of separated plutonium and high-level vitrified waste
- Located on the Cotentin Peninsula tip — exposed to Atlantic weather, facing the English Channel
- If hit: releases plutonium, cesium, strontium, americium, and fission products on a scale that makes Sellafield look moderate
- Fallout pattern depends on wind: east → across Channel to southern England; south → Brittany and the Loire; northeast → Belgium and Netherlands
- The Cotentin Peninsula itself becomes a permanent exclusion zone comparable to Northern Ireland in the story

## Summary: What Survives in Europe?

If France's nuclear sites are all hit alongside the UK sites already established in the story:

| Region | Status |
|---|---|
| France (most) | Uninhabitable. Rivers carry contamination to all coasts. |
| Southern Ireland (below Waterford-Limerick) | Survivable — protected by Atlantic distance and winds |
| Western Brittany | Possibly survivable (marginal, depends on La Hague wind) |
| Deep Pyrenees / Basque | Possibly survivable |
| Corsica | Possibly survivable (island buffer) |
| Switzerland (underground) | Shelters protect people; surface contaminated |
| Germany | Devastated from French + own reactor fallout |
| Belgium / Luxembourg / Netherlands | Devastated |
| Spain (most) | Partially affected northeast; interior/south possibly viable |
| Portugal | Relatively protected by distance and Atlantic position |
| Scandinavia | Depends on own reactor status and wind; Norway's Atlantic coast may be okay |
| Mediterranean | Contaminated semi-enclosed sea; slow recovery |
| North Africa | Marginal contamination from Mediterranean; mostly distance-protected |

## Implications for the Story

- Southern Ireland's status as a rare safe zone is even more exceptional when you factor in France
- The Ireland character lives in one of the few genuinely habitable places left in Western Europe
- The Catholic network operating from southern Ireland has strategic value far beyond religion — it's one of the few functioning institutional structures in a devastated continent
- Any European coastal trade route must navigate contaminated waterways
- The Mediterranean is a dead sea — this affects any connections between Europe and the Middle East/North Africa
- Portugal and western Spain may be another functional zone (Atlantic-facing, distance from French sites) — possible trade partners for southern Ireland
- Switzerland's bunker population becomes an interesting story element: a nation that survived underground while the surface died
